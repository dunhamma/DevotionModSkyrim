# Orc

> There is one god. He was thrown out. So were you. The question is how you carry the code when the world won't make room for it.

## Who They Worship

Orcs worship **Malacath** — and only Malacath. This isn't a limitation. It's a theological statement. Malacath is the spurned god of the spurned people. The Aedra don't want him. The Daedra mock him. And in that rejection, he built something: a code. A way of living that transforms exile into purpose, humiliation into strength, and being unwanted into being *unbreakable*.

Aedric worship alongside Malacath is theologically incoherent. There is no Orc pantheon. There are no secondary gods in any meaningful sense. The question isn't "which god?" — it's "how do you carry this one god's code in the life you actually live?"

## How Devotion Works for Orcs

**Worship type:** Single-Core (Mono) — one religion, three social modes of expressing it.

**Setup choice:** Your lived social mode — not a different theology, but a different *circumstance* for the same faith:

| Mode | What it means | Devotion ceiling |
|------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Stronghold Orc** | Full expression of Malacath's order. Forge, shrine, chief, community. | Highest |
| **City Orc** | Private fidelity under public compromise. Quality labor, dignity in mixed society. | Lower |
| **Legion / Service / Exile** | Honor under foreign discipline. Contract, oath, endurance under humiliation. | Lower |

**There is no separate focused-primary deity layer.** Deepening comes through mode-specific Malacath excellence, not through choosing a different god.

**Mode hierarchy:** Stronghold is the full-expression baseline. City and Legion/Exile are partial or compromised expressions of the same code. Their compensation is broader survivability and flexibility — not equal devotional depth.

Stronghold rewards are mostly stable and permanent: you live inside the code's full infrastructure. City and Legion/Exile rewards are more dynamic and situational. They can be sharper in the right moment, but they depend on context because those Orcs are carrying the code without the stronghold around them.

## The Three Modes

### Stronghold Orc — Full Code
*The forge is a shrine. The chief's word is law. The stronghold is the world Malacath built for his people.*

- **What you do:** Achieve forge excellence. Keep oaths. Provide for the community. Face challenges head-on and prove strength through accepted trials. Aid strongholds. Complete The Cursed Tribe.
- **What you get:** The highest devotion ceiling of any Orc mode. Blessings of communal belonging, forge-prayer, and proven strength.
- **The feel:** An Orc living inside the code as it was meant to be lived — complete, demanding, and rewarding.
- **Entry gate:** Blood-Kin acceptance is the main gateway. Without stronghold recognition, you can't fully practice stronghold faith.

### City Orc — The Compromise
*Malacath still sees you. But the stronghold doesn't.*

- **What you do:** Maintain quality labor and dignity in mixed society. Keep Orc identity without full stronghold structure. Navigate non-Orc institutions with self-respect intact.
- **What you get:** Situational favors of compromise dignity — recognition that private fidelity matters even without full communal practice.
- **The feel:** An Orc who carries the code quietly. Not betrayal — *adaptation*. But the ceiling is lower because the infrastructure isn't there.
- **Note:** City Orc is the default bridge state for any Orc not recognized by a stronghold. It's where you land when you haven't earned stronghold standing.

### Legion / Service / Exile Orc — The Endurance
*The contract still matters. The oath still binds. Even when the people you serve would spit on your shrine.*

- **What you do:** Complete service contracts and Legion milestones under unequal conditions. Maintain honor despite humiliation. Prove martial competence under foreign discipline.
- **What you get:** Situational blessings of endurance and persistence — Malacath rewards carrying the code where no one else will help you, especially when the burden is visible.
- **The feel:** An Orc whose devotion is *survival with dignity* when everything around them says they're disposable. The burden is high. The ceiling is not.

## Self-Made Community

City and Legion/Exile Orcs can build a community around themselves. This is not a replacement stronghold; it is a hard-won place where the code has witnesses. Visiting an invested location gives a devotion boost as it develops from empty, to established, to thriving.

The preferred implementation reads NPC disposition around the chosen community. If that proves too heavy for first release, faction-favor proxies carry the same shape: stranger, acquaintance, friend, community member.

## Spiritual Authority

Across all modes, Orc spiritual life is guided by **Shamans**, **Witch Doctors**, and **Wise Women**. Their presence varies by mode:
- Stronghold: Strong, active presence. Direct spiritual guidance.
- City: Weaker. Remembered rather than immediate.
- Legion/Exile: Rarest. Internalized. The shaman's voice comes from memory.

## Mode Switching

Mode changes are socially and religiously consequential — not casual swaps:

- **Stronghold** is hardest to enter and re-enter. Blood-Kin is the gateway.
- **City** is the easiest bridge state — the default landing.
- **Legion/Exile** is easy to fall into through circumstance but hard to leave cleanly once it's defined you for a long time.

## The Daedric Question

For Orcs, most Daedric worship is **foreign** — not hostile in the way Altmer feel it, but simply *irrelevant*. The code is Malacath. Other powers don't have a framework inside Orc theology.

- **Malacath** — Native. The entire Orc religious system.
- **Boethiah** ��� Hostile. Deepest rival. The betrayer of Trinimac (Malacath's original form). Load-bearing hostility.
- **Auri-El** — Hostile. Trinimac's betrayal narrative.
- **Hircine** — Taboo, but *conditionally defensible* through proven strength and control (see Curse States).
- **Molag Bal** — Taboo. Dependency contradicts the code.
- **Nocturnal** — Taboo. Cunning can tempt exiles but isn't Malacath code.
- **Most other princes** — Foreign. Outsider paths that don't fit the code.

### Trinimac

A note on Trinimac — the ancestor-hero Malacath *was* before Boethiah's betrayal. In 4E 201 Skyrim, Trinimac is a rare ideological pressure rather than a normal player path. Historically important, politically charged, but not the everyday religious function of Skyrim-era Orc life. The mod acknowledges Trinimac without building a full devotional lane for him.

## Curse States

**Vampire:**
Near-total collapse. Exile from stronghold acceptance. Malacath devotion becomes hollow or nonfunctional. No real positive Orc theological replacement exists. Vampirism is *dependency* — and dependency is the thing Malacath's code exists to reject.

**Werewolf:**
Conditionally defensible. Unlike most races, Orc werewolfism isn't automatically catastrophic — it's a *demanding test*:
- If strength and control are proven, some stronghold tolerance is possible
- Still under constant pressure, never a free positive
- Must demonstrate discipline rather than losing control
- The beast must serve the code, not replace it

This makes Orc one of the few races where werewolfism has a viable (if demanding) theological accommodation. It's not comfortable. But it's possible in a way that vampire is not.

## Playing This Race — What to Expect

Playing an Orc in Devotion feels like the question is always **"how fully can you live inside the code?"** — not which code to follow.

The mode system means your social circumstance matters as much as your actions. A Stronghold Orc with Blood-Kin recognition has access to the full depth of Malacath's devotion. A City Orc carries the same faith at lower volume, compensating with broader flexibility. A Legion/Exile Orc endures the heaviest burden with the lowest ceiling.

The forge is prayer. The oath is scripture. The challenge is worship. Every test you face — whether it's a stronghold crisis, a foreign contract, or just maintaining dignity in a city that barely tolerates you — is an expression of what Malacath demands.

What makes Orc distinctive in the mod is the *absence of choice at the divine level*. Where every other race asks "which god?", the Orc asks "how much of the god's world can I build around me in these conditions?" The answer changes everything about what devotion looks like — but never what it points toward.
