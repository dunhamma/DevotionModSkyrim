# Redguard

> The dead are watching. The blade remembers. The way must be made.

## Who They Worship

Redguard religion is the Yokudan pantheon — gods who sailed with the Ra Gada from lost Yokuda and whose names are not interchangeable with Imperial equivalents even when outsiders pretend they are. Tu'whacca guides the dead. Ruptga (Tall Papa) charts the path. Leki and Onsi teach the blade. Tava carries the wind. HoonDing makes the way when no way exists.

Underneath all specific god-worship lies the **ancestor reverence layer** — a constant awareness of the dead as witnesses to how you live. This isn't a god to worship. It's a gravity that pulls on everything else.

And above it all sits **Satakal** — the Worldskin, the great cycle of creation and destruction that everything participates in whether they acknowledge it or not.

In 4E 201, a Redguard in Skyrim is also defined by *sect identity* — Crown (orthodox preservationist), Forebear (adaptive pragmatist), or Ash'abah (funerary outcast).

## How Devotion Works for Redguards

**Worship type:** Sect-Layered Pantheon — your sect shapes how you worship, and ancestors are always present beneath.

**Setup choice:** Choose your societal-religious frame:

| Sect | Character | Ancestor Weight |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Crown** | Preservation, orthodoxy, sacred martial inheritance | Strong |
| **Forebear** | Adaptation, public life, pragmatic survival in foreign spaces | Moderate |
| **Ash'abah** | Funerary duty, undead-cleansing, loyalty to the dead at social cost | Very strong |

All three remain inside the same Yokudan religious universe. They differ by emphasis, daily interpretation, and social burden.

**Broad worship** within your sect reaches **Faithful**. **Devoted** requires focused commitment to a primary deity.

**Always-on ancestor reverence:** Strongest around death, tombs, undead, legacy, and funerary duty. Not a separate path — an interpretive layer that colors everything.

## The Sect System

### Crown — Keeper of the Old Way
*Preservation. Bearing. The sacred inheritance kept intact in exile.*

- **What you do:** Maintain Yokudan tradition. Honor martial discipline. Preserve identity against assimilation. Make sect-defining quest choices that reinforce orthodoxy.
- **What you get:** Strong ancestor layer blessings. Recognition of preserved lineage.
- **The feel:** A Redguard whose exile strengthens rather than weakens their identity.
- **Focused deity options:** Satakal, Tu'whacca, Ruptga, Leki, Onsi, HoonDing (situational)

### Forebear — Adaptive Survivor
*Living Redguard identity in the world as it is, not as Yokuda was.*

- **What you do:** Take mercenary contracts. Negotiate mixed-society survival. Maintain Redguard identity while engaging with foreign institutions. Complete service and diplomacy quests.
- **What you get:** Blessings of adaptive strength — favor for surviving well in foreign spaces.
- **The feel:** A Redguard who carries the gods forward by living among outsiders without losing themselves.
- **Focused deity options:** Satakal, Tu'whacca, Tava, Leki, Zeht, HoonDing, Morwha (social option)
- **Note:** Forebear allows syncretic reading of Imperial parallels (Arkay/Tu'whacca, etc.) but Yokudan names remain primary.

### Ash'abah — Bearer of the Unclean Duty
*Someone must clean the dead. Someone must carry the stigma.*

- **What you do:** Cleanse undead. Honor burial rites. Complete Hall of the Dead and anti-necromancy quests. Carry the funerary duty that others won't touch.
- **What you get:** The strongest devotion potential around death and funerary duty of any Redguard sect. But lowered social standing — the duty costs you.
- **The feel:** A Redguard whose holiness looks like pollution to everyone else. The truest servant of Tu'whacca, bearing the disgust of those they protect.
- **Focused deity options:** Tu'whacca, Satakal, HoonDing, Ruptga (harder/less common)
- **Note:** Available at setup AND as a later drift/unlock path. A Redguard can become Ash'abah through sustained funerary action.

## Paths of Devotion (Focused Deities)

### Tu'whacca — Guardian of the Far Shores
*The death-keeper. Not Arkay — older, deeper, Yokudan.*

- **What you do:** Cleanse undead. Maintain tomb order. Complete anti-necromancy quests. Honor proper burial wherever you encounter death.
- **What you get:** Religious privileges tied to death-duty — recognition in sacred spaces, protection from corruption.
- **The feel:** A Redguard who makes sure the dead reach the Far Shores.
- **Strongest for:** Ash'abah, but valid across all sects.

### Leki / Onsi — The Blade Saints
*Martial discipline as divine practice.*

- **What you do:** Fight with discipline, not desperation. Complete honorable martial quest resolutions. Refuse cowardly advantage. Prove blade-grace through skill rather than body count.
- **What you get:** Blessings of martial virtue — combat conduct recognized as worship.
- **The feel:** A Redguard whose sword-arm is a prayer and whose discipline is devotion.
- **Note:** This is *not* a generic combat farming path. Body count doesn't matter. Conduct does.

### HoonDing — The Make-Way God
*For impossible odds. For moments when no path exists and one must be carved.*

- **What you do:** Overcome impossible situations. Complete breakthrough quests where everything was stacked against you. Make the way where there was none.
- **What you get:** Rare, powerful favors — strongest in moments of genuine breakthrough.
- **The feel:** A Redguard who invokes the path-maker only at the threshold of the impossible.
- **Note:** Intentionally narrow and dramatic, but attainable for active adventurers. Dragon fights, named creature victories, major quest climaxes, and other "big win" moments can all count when they truly read as clearing an impossible obstacle. HoonDing doesn't notice ordinary victories.

### Satakal — The Worldskin
*The great cycle. Creation and destruction as a single breath.*

- **What you do:** Acknowledge the cycle. Accept change without fighting it. Complete quests that involve endings becoming beginnings.
- **What you get:** Blessings tied to cosmic awareness — protection during transition, steadiness during upheaval.
- **The feel:** A Redguard who sees the wheel turning and doesn't flinch.

### Tava — The Wind
*Movement, wayfinding, the breath between places.*

- **What you do:** Travel with purpose. Complete journey and discovery quests. Follow the wind rather than fighting it.
- **What you get:** Travel-facing blessings — favor in motion, guidance toward what matters.
- **The feel:** A Redguard carried by purpose across foreign lands.

## The Daedric Question

Redguard theology has specific, named hostilities:

- **Molag Bal** — Hostile. The strongest Redguard hostility. Breaks everything: Far Shores, Tu'whacca's guidance, mortality order.
- **Malacath/Malooc** — Hostile. Cautionary enemy-god in Yokudan memory.
- **Sep** — Hostile (but not Daedric per se). The cautionary serpent, failed creation, banished error.
- **Mehrunes Dagon** — Hostile. Foreign destroyer opposing way-making and civic survival.
- **Hircine** — Taboo (Curse access). No true Redguard home for beast-shape.
- **Most other princes** — Foreign. Outsider paths that don't map to Yokudan devotion.

Redguards don't have the Breton's cultural legibility with Daedric forces. There's no "intelligible but risky" middle ground for most princes — they're simply *outside the frame*.

## Curse States

**Vampire:**
Near-total collapse. No meaningful positive Yokudan substitute path. The Far Shores destiny is broken while the curse remains active. Sect identity persists as memory and grief, not active religious function. This is one of the worst vampire landings in the mod.

On cure: Redemption is possible. Restoration happens first through Tu'whacca — proper mortality, ancestor order, right re-entry into the cycle. Only *after* basic restoration may you re-dedicate to a specific primary god.

**Werewolf:**
Sect and god structure remain accessible but strained. No true Hircine-integrated Redguard path opens. The condition is theologically homeless — not destroyed like vampirism, but *without a frame*. Favor and interpretation strain. You carry Malacath's question (exile, strength, burden) without his answer.

## Playing This Race — What to Expect

Playing a Redguard in Devotion feels like **duty and inheritance** — what your sect demands, what your ancestors watch for, and how your specific gods reward the life you lead.

The sect choice is the defining frame. A Crown Redguard feels the weight of preservation — every compromise is a small betrayal. A Forebear feels the tension of adaptation — is participating in Imperial society survival or surrender? An Ash'abah feels the cost of holiness — you do the work no one else will, and they despise you for it.

The ancestor layer gives everything a witnessing quality. It's not that specific acts trigger specific rewards — it's that the dead are *present* behind every choice. Tu'whacca and the Far Shores make death meaningful rather than arbitrary. HoonDing makes impossible victories feel cosmically endorsed rather than merely lucky.

The distinctive Redguard feel is that your gods have *Yokudan names* even when the world tries to call them something else. This isn't a reskinned Imperial experience — it's an older tradition maintaining itself in foreign territory.
