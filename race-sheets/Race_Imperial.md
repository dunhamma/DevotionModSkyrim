# Imperial

**Status:** Player-facing companion sheet. Implementation authority lives in `PDV_RaceDesign_Imperial.md`, `PDV_TargetEndStates_1.0.md`, and `references/PDV_RaceArchitecture_DesignReference.md`; reward numbers remain tunable.

> Faith is civic duty. But duty has a conscience — and sometimes conscience demands treason.

## Who They Worship

Imperials worship the Nine Divines as a single, coherent civic religion. Different gods serve different life stations — Arkay for the funeral, Mara for the marriage, Zenithar for the merchant, Stendarr for the judge. This is religion as infrastructure, woven into law, commerce, and public life.

But there is a fracture in 4E 201. The White-Gold Concordat banned Talos worship, and now every Imperial faces a question their ancestors never had to: do you obey the law, or do you obey your faith?

## How Devotion Works for Imperials

**Worship type:** Pantheon (Poly) — the Nine Divines, with a political rift running through them.

**No setup choice needed.** Imperials begin in broad Nine Divines worship automatically. Broad worship can reach **Faithful** — civic observance is culturally normal and respected.

**Focused worship** is triggered the same way as Nords — sustained alignment with a single god's domain eventually draws an offer. Accept to pursue **Devoted**.

**The unique wrinkle:** The Concordat Standing track. This political meter runs beneath your devotion, tracking where you fall on the Talos question. It's not a separate system — it *modifies* how your devotion works.

## The Concordat Standing

This is the defining mechanic of the Imperial race. A hidden reputation that shifts based on your choices about Talos:

**Open Defiant** — You actively resist the ban. Protect worshippers, hide shrines, fight Thalmor. Talos devotion comes faster and stronger. But the Thalmor hunt you.

**Private Defiant** — You resist quietly. Refuse to report worshippers, activate shrines when no one watches. Modest Talos boost. Thalmor suspicious.

**Uncommitted** — You haven't taken a side. Standard devotion rates. Thalmor ignore you.

**Public Compliant** — You enforce the ban. Report worshippers, cooperate with Thalmor. Talos devotion slows significantly. Thalmor friendly.

**Concordat Enforcer** — You are a tool of suppression. Talos is nearly unreachable. But there's a deeper cost: enabling mass persecution weakens your connection to *Arkay* (improper death rites at scale) and *Stendarr* (mercy abandoned). Compliance isn't free — it corrodes other divine relationships too.

The Concordat Standing shifts through quest choices — Civil War decisions, encounters with Thalmor, what you do when you find a hidden shrine. Single actions move it in small increments. Crossing between states requires sustained behavior, not a single dramatic act.

The middle is intentionally wide. Most normal play sits in **Uncommitted**, where the system watches but does not force a stance from one incidental choice. You only become publicly compliant or meaningfully defiant through a sustained pattern.

Walking back from an extreme is possible, but harder at the edges. An Open Defiant or Concordat Enforcer Imperial needs consistent counter-behavior and, eventually, a story-caliber act to return fully to the center. The system supports real repentance or compromise without letting the character flip identities casually.

## Paths of Devotion

### Talos — The Forbidden God
*For those who keep faith in secret or in defiance.*

- **What you do:** Activate hidden shrines. Protect worshippers from Thalmor. Side with the Stormcloaks (unusual for an Imperial, but powerful). Refuse to comply.
- **What you get:** Religious privileges that reward dangerous faith. The more you risk, the more Talos offers.
- **The feel:** An Imperial whose faith makes them a criminal in their own empire.

### Arkay — The Death-Keeper
*For those who honor the passage between life and death.*

- **What you do:** Visit Halls of the Dead. Complete anti-necromancy quests. Ensure proper burial. Fight the undead as religious duty.
- **What you get:** Blessings tied to the cycle of life and death — protection from corruption, recognition in sacred spaces.
- **The feel:** An Imperial who serves the dead so they may rest properly.

### Stendarr — The Merciful
*For those who choose restraint when persecution would be easier.*

- **What you do:** Spare enemies when you could kill. Choose mercy in quest resolutions. Protect the persecuted. Refuse cruelty even when it's politically advantageous.
- **What you get:** Favors tied to righteous restraint — strongest when mercy costs you something.
- **The feel:** An Imperial who believes the Empire's strength should be compassion, not control.

### Mara — The Civic Heart
*For those who build social bonds and care for community.*

- **What you do:** Marry. Participate in charity. Complete community restoration quests. Build social infrastructure.
- **What you get:** Blessings tied to social connection, household, and civic care.
- **The feel:** An Imperial whose religion is lived through kindness.

### Akatosh — The Civic Order
*For those who serve time, law, and continuity.*

- **What you do:** Sustain a long devotion streak. Complete Legion objectives. Uphold civic order through quest choices.
- **What you get:** Blessings tied to stability, endurance, and the long arc of Imperial civilization.
- **The feel:** An Imperial who worships through loyal service.

### Concordat Compliance Lane
*For those who choose obedience over conscience.*

- **What you do:** Report Talos worshippers. Cooperate with Thalmor. Enforce the ban actively.
- **What you get:** Political safety and Thalmor alliance. But theological cost — Arkay and Stendarr both suffer.
- **The feel:** An Imperial who traded faith for order. Whether that's pragmatism or cowardice is the question.

## The Daedric Question

Imperials view Daedric worship as **anti-civic** — a rejection of the public religious order that holds society together. It's not the cosmic horror a Nord feels, but a kind of social treason.

- **Most princes** are taboo — intelligible as temptation but corrosive to public virtue.
- **Meridia** is tolerated for her anti-undead utility (fits the Arkay frame).
- **Nocturnal** stains public standing through the Thieves Guild's Nightingale oath.
- **Molag Bal** under vampirism causes total Divine collapse — civic faith cannot accommodate the undead.

## Curse States

**Vampire:** The Nine Divines path halts entirely. This isn't a slowdown — it's a full stop. Civic religion requires being *alive* in the community. The Concordat Standing becomes religiously irrelevant (though politically you're still in danger). On cure, devotion resumes but starts from a lower floor. The rupture leaves a mark.

**Werewolf:** Nine Divines devotion continues but at reduced effectiveness. Social and civic-facing devotion weakens. There's no Imperial framework for Hircine — no theological home for the beast. You become spiritually homeless without fully losing your faith. Just... isolated within it.

## Playing This Race — What to Expect

Playing an Imperial in Devotion feels like navigating a political minefield with your soul at stake. The system constantly asks: what do you believe, and what are you willing to pay for it?

The Concordat Standing is the spine of the experience. Every encounter with Talos, the Thalmor, or the Civil War moves your needle. And the system is designed so that compliance has *real theological cost* — you don't get to be a Concordat Enforcer and also maintain a perfect relationship with Stendarr. The Empire's compromise corrodes the very faith it claims to protect.

The richest Imperial playthrough involves picking a side and feeling the consequences ripple through every divine relationship you have.
