# Devotion - Blessings and Penalties (Numbers Reference)

This is the companion "numbers" article for the per-race guides. The race guides
tell you what your gods want and what their gifts feel like; this one lists the
exact stat values, the Champion special powers, and what each god counts as an
offense. If you just want to play, read your race's guide - you never need this
page. It is here for players who like to see the maths.

All values are current beta numbers and may be tuned before release. Where a race
guide and this page ever disagree, trust the in-game spell description.

## How the Tiers Work

- **Focused (patron) ladder:** Seeker at 25 piety, Devoted at 50, Champion at 85. Only the highest tier you have reached is active - the tiers do not stack.
- **Broad worship** (honoring several gods at once, or a whole sect or pantheon) tops out at the Faithful tier, roughly equivalent to Devoted. Reaching Champion always means letting one single god clearly lead.
- **One family at a time.** While a focused god's gifts are active, your always-on cultural blessing steps aside, and only that one god's ladder is live.
- **One save per race.** At most one of your Champion-tier gifts is a scripted "cheat-death" power (see below). Every other Champion tier is a strong passive; its "special" is the recognition itself - your patron naming you - not an active proc.

## Champion Special Powers

These are the scripted powers that ride on top of the Champion stat lines. They
are the exception, not the rule: most Champion tiers are passive stats. Each one
below is verified against the shipped records.

| Race | God | Power | What it does |
|------|-----|-------|--------------|
| Khajiit | Baan Dar | Baan Dar Remembers | Once per day, when a blow would drop you (health falls to a fifth or lower), Baan Dar slips you out of death's hand - you are put back on your feet instead of dying. Khajiit's single cheat-death. |
| Nord | Shor | Shor Remembers | Once per day, a killing blow becomes a last stand - Shor pulls you back from the edge and you survive it. Nord's single cheat-death. |
| Redguard | HoonDing | HoonDing Makes Way | Once per day, a lethal blow is refused - HoonDing opens the road back from the edge and you keep your feet. Redguard's single cheat-death. |
| Redguard | Tu'whacca | Death-rite restoration | From Devoted upward, keeping a death-rite - putting the restless dead to rest, or carrying a Far Shores token - restores a flat measure of health once a day: about 30 at Devoted, 50 at Champion. A flat restore, so it is felt even under Requiem. This is a heal, not a cheat-death, so it does not count as the race's save. |
| Dunmer | The Ancestors | The Ancestors Watch | Armed by keeping the home prayer (pray at your ancestral urn or hearth). For that day, if you are brought to the brink, once the ancestors pull you back. Disarmed at dawn, so it is re-earned daily. Dunmer's single cheat-death. |
| Bosmer | Baan Dar (Bandit Road) | Baan Dar Opens the Gap | On the Bandit Road, once a day a killing blow becomes an opening - you slip it and break away. Bosmer's single cheat-death. |
| Argonian | Sithis (the Void) | Void near-death surge | While the Void is your active focus, once a day near death the Nothing lends a brief surge - a burst of stamina and momentum to fight your way clear. |
| Orc | Malacath (any focus) | The Code Holds | With Malacath's regard, once in a fight when near death the Code holds - a flat burst of health (more at Devoted and up) keeps you standing. |

Altmer, Breton, and Imperial have no scripted power: every one of their Champion
tiers is a strong passive, and the reward is the recognition itself.

A cheat-death fires at most once a day (Orc's holds once per fight) and only when
your health has already dropped to a fifth or less; it will not save you from a
single overwhelming hit that skips that window.

**What offends your gods:**

- **Malacath** judges by the Code, not by kindness: murder of the helpless (medium) and assault of the innocent (medium) are weakness, and theft and a soft inn bed are small failings of the Code.
- Malacath cares little for necromancy or Daedric bargains as such - the Code is about strength kept and kin unbroken. Breaking faith with kin, or a curse the Code cannot abide, is covered in the Orc guide's own sections.
