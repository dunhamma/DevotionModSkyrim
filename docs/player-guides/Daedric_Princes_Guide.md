# The Daedric Princes

In Devotion, your character builds a religious life through the deeds they do, the god they honor, and the world they move through. Most of that life points toward the safe, ordinary gods of your people. This guide is about the other road: the sixteen Daedric Princes, the powerful and dangerous spirits you can deal with no matter what race you are.

If you have not read the "How Devotion Works" primer yet, read it first. This guide assumes you already understand piety, the tiers, and how devotion is tracked. Everything here builds on that.

## Walking the Daedric Road

Every race can deal with a Daedric Prince. None of them can do it safely.

Honoring a Prince is never the civic, respectable path. It is not like lighting a candle at a temple of Mara or making an honest day's offering to Zenithar. A Prince's regard is a bargain. You give something to get something, and the something you give is rarely small.

That bargain shows up the same way your normal devotion does. The Prince notices what you do. You climb from being barely noticed, to being noticed, to being claimed. The piety bar still fills the same way, separately for each Prince, capped at about the same gain each day. But the meaning underneath is darker. Where a god might reward kindness, a Prince may reward cruelty, theft, ruin, or madness. The road runs the same direction. It just runs through worse country.

A few things stay true no matter which Prince you walk toward:

- The deed has to fit. A Prince only cares about acts inside their own sphere. Killing the helpless means nothing to Meridia but pleases Molag Bal. Reading forbidden books means nothing to Malacath but feeds Hermaeus Mora.
- Repeating the exact same deed earns less each time, just like ordinary devotion. Variety matters far more than grinding.
- You can usually walk back out. But walking out leaves a mark.

## The Sixteen Princes

These are the sixteen Princes you can reach in Skyrim. Each rewards a different kind of life. Here is what each one wants from you, in a single line.

- **Azura** - twilight, fate, and prophecy. She favors mercy shown to the cursed and outcast, foresight, and the dawn-and-dusk watch. Her Star is her gift.
- **Boethiah** - struggle and the overthrow of false authority. She rewards proving yourself through open battle, hard self-betterment, betrayal as a test, and the culling of the unworthy.
- **Mephala** - secrets, webs, and the hidden blade. She favors stealth, manipulation, quiet murder, and crossing into forbidden places.
- **Malacath** - the harsh code of the spurned. He rewards strength proven in fair fight, the work of the forge, and enduring hardship. He despises striking the defenseless and the thief's sneaking.
- **Meridia** - the living light and the burning of the dead. She rewards destroying the undead and rejecting necromancy. Raising the dead is the deepest offense against her.
- **Hircine** - the great hunt. He favors the kill of worthy prey, the chase, the night under open sky, and the beast's shape. He is one of the two Princes reached mainly through a curse.
- **Molag Bal** - domination and the breaking of the weak. He rewards cruelty to the helpless, taking by force, and the violation of soul and body. He is the other curse-linked Prince.
- **Nocturnal** - shadow, luck, and debt. She rewards the picked lock, the quiet theft, and the unseen way. Her gifts always come with a price that comes due.
- **Hermaeus Mora** - forbidden knowledge endlessly hoarded. He favors reading lore and spell tomes, discovering hidden places, and learning every secret there is.
- **Mehrunes Dagon** - destruction, ruin, and revolution. He rewards cutting down what stands, breaking the mighty, and terror. Building and preserving offend him.
- **Sheogorath** - madness and the breaking of routine. He delights in the absurd, the novel, and the unpredictable. The orderly and the dull bore him.
- **Namira** - revulsion, decay, and the outcast's hunger. She is reached through cannibalism, the embrace of rot, and solidarity with rejected things.
- **Sanguine** - excess and indulgence. He favors the feast, the drink, and the revel. Sober restraint cuts his party short.
- **Clavicus Vile** - the bargain and the wish. He rewards the deal struck, power claimed by terms rather than toil, and the loophole. He prefers a bargain to honest work or simple theft.
- **Peryite** - plague, order, and the lowest task. He favors patient humble labor kept in its proper place, and the bearing of disease. Curing affliction undoes the order he imposes.
- **Vaermina** - dreams, nightmares, and stolen memory. She visits the sleeper, harvests the dream, and feeds on fear and forbidden lore.

## How a Pact Works

Reaching a Prince's regard follows the same climb as any devotion in this mod. You start as no one to them. As your fitting deeds add up, you become noticed (Seeker), then bound to their service (Devoted), then fully claimed as their own (Champion). The piety bar, the daily cap, and the anti-grind rule all work exactly as they do for the ordinary gods. The mod simply uses darker words for these stages when you read your standing in the Devotion panel: the Prince has "touched" you, then "bound" you, then "claimed" you.

But four things make a Daedric pact different from honoring an ordinary god. Keep all four in mind before you start down this road.

**A pact needs a commitment.** A Prince will notice you drifting toward their sphere, but noticing is not the same as serving. Real progress usually begins only when you make a clear commitment: taking up the Prince's artifact, finishing their quest their way, joining their faction, or in two cases, being cursed into their grasp. Until then, you are only tempted, not bound.

**There is always a PRICE.** Every Prince's gift carries a cost. The cost fits the Prince. Hermaeus Mora's knowledge gnaws at your own will. Sanguine's revels make you unreliable. Molag Bal's power corrupts. Refusing to pay a Prince's price slows or caps how far you can rise with them. The gift is never free, and the bill always fits the giver.

**There is STIGMA.** Your people may shun this. Most races have no comfortable place for Daedric worship, and the mod will tell you when you have crossed a line, through a message, a change in how you are treated, or a shift in your standing. Some Princes are merely frowned upon. Others are a rupture with everything your character was raised to be.

**There is an EXIT, and it leaves RESIDUE.** You can usually renounce a Prince. Curing your curse, rejecting the artifact, turning to a rival, or simply living against the Prince's sphere for a long stretch can break the claim. But renouncing leaves a mark. The Prince's hold loosens rather than vanishing clean: lingering stigma, a lower starting point if you ever return, or a renewed pull of temptation. The mark fades over time and never permanently ruins your character. It is residue, not a ruined save.

## Who Can Approach Whom

A Prince means something different depending on who you are. The same pact that is a curse for a Nord is a homecoming for an Orc. This section sorts the Princes for each race into four kinds of fit:

- **Natural fit** - part of, or close to, the race's own religious world. The path uses the race's own worship language rather than feeling like an outside bargain.
- **Tolerated** - allowed under narrow, practical conditions, usually for a single useful purpose.
- **Taboo or hostile** - spiritually and socially dangerous, ranging from frowned-upon to a full rupture with the race's worldview.
- **Curse only** - reached chiefly by becoming a werewolf or a vampire.

Two routes are curse-linked for everyone, so call them out plainly:

- **Hircine comes through lycanthropy.** Becoming a werewolf opens Hircine's road regardless of how your people would normally feel about him. This is the werewolf's Prince.
- **Molag Bal comes through vampirism.** Becoming a vampire opens Molag Bal's road the same way. This is the vampire's Prince.

When a curse opens one of these roads, curing it later reopens the door to recovery and return.

**Race by race:**

- **Nord** - Mostly taboo. Hircine is the legible exception, reached through werewolf service in the Companions. Meridia is tolerated for her hatred of the undead. Molag Bal is a severe rupture, and vampirism that opens his road also closes the door to Sovngarde.
- **Imperial** - Mostly civic taboo. Meridia is tolerated for anti-undead duty. Nocturnal's road runs through crime and oath, outside respectable Imperial life. Mehrunes Dagon is especially hated, since Imperials remember the Oblivion Crisis.
- **Breton** - Their witchcraft makes some Daedric contact legible rather than alien. Hircine, Namira, and Hermaeus Mora are the cleanest inherited pressures. Meridia is tolerated outside the witchcraft frame.
- **Dunmer** - Azura, Boethiah, and Mephala are a natural fit; the Dunmer honor these three as their own Good Daedra, in their own ancestral language, not as outside pacts. The other Princes remain testing, adversarial, or foreign.
- **Altmer** - Almost everything Daedric reads as apostasy. The Altmer have no safe road here at all. See the caution below.
- **Khajiit** - Azurah is a natural fit, woven into their moon-lit faith. A few others can carry cultural meaning where the Khajiit's lunar world makes room for them, but no Prince should overwrite that lunar foundation.
- **Bosmer** - Hircine is the main Daedric pressure, through the hunt, lycanthropy, and the old shadow of the Wild Hunt.
- **Redguard** - Mostly foreign and hostile. Meridia can be tolerated for cleansing the undead, but never in place of the duty owed to Tu'whacca and the ancestors. Malacath is a cautionary, hostile figure to a Redguard.
- **Orc** - Malacath is not a pact at all; he is the core of Orc religion, honored as the orc-father through code, exile, the forge, and vengeance. Every other Prince is a threat, a test, or an outside bargain, and Boethiah is an especially costly one.
- **Argonian** - Daedric contact is mostly foreign and weakens the Hist-and-community life that holds an Argonian together. Sithis is not a Daedric Prince and sits apart from all of this. Molag Bal and Hircine still enter through the vampire and werewolf curses.

## A Word of Caution

Daedric power is real. The gifts are sharper and more specialized than what the ordinary gods give, and when the right narrow moment arrives, a Prince's favor can be exactly the edge you needed. But it is rarely free, and the price always fits the Prince. Some races, the Altmer most of all, have no safe road to it whatever, and for them every Daedric step is a step into apostasy. Walk this road with your eyes open. The Princes remember what you owe them, and so does the mod.

Note: these are current beta values and may be tuned before release. The exact strength of Daedric blessings, prices, and stigma effects is still being balanced, so treat the descriptions above as the shape of each path rather than a fixed number.
