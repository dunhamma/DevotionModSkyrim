# How Devotion Works

Devotion is a mod about your character's religious life. The gods of Tamriel
notice how you actually play, and they answer back. The longer and more
sincerely you follow one of them, the more present that relationship becomes:
small blessings, the occasional moment of grace in a fitting situation, and in
time, real recognition.

This page explains the systems once, so the individual race guides can stay
short. If a race guide says "see the How Devotion Works primer," this is the
page it means. Read this first, then read your race's guide for what makes
your people specific.

## Piety

Piety is the single most important word in this mod. It is the measure of how
close you are to a god. Think of it as standing, faith, or favor in that god's
eyes.

A few things are true about piety, and everything else follows from them:

- **Piety is tracked separately for every god.** You can be a stranger to Mara and a near-Champion of Talos at the same time. Honoring one god does not fill some shared meter; each relationship is its own.
- **Each god's piety runs from 0 to 85.** That is the whole bar. When you open the Devotion panel and see a god's bar filling up, it is filling from 0 toward 85.
- **You earn piety with a god by doing the things that god cares about.** A god of the hunt notices you hunting. A god of mercy notices you sparing a beaten enemy. A god of the dead notices you tending graves and putting down the walking dead. The race guides tell you which deeds matter to which gods.
- **There is a daily limit.** You can raise a single god's piety by only about 4.3 points per day, no matter how much you do. This is deliberate. Faith is built over time, not crammed into one afternoon.

Because of that daily cap, reaching the top of the bar is a journey of days,
not a grind of repeated clicks. With normal play (one or two devotional acts a
day) it takes roughly 30 to 45 days to carry a god all the way to the top. If
you focus hard and hit the daily cap often, it can be done in about 20 days.
Either way, it is meant to feel earned.

## The Three Tiers

As a god's piety climbs, you cross thresholds. Each one is a tier, and each
tier deepens the relationship.

- **None** - Below 25 piety. The god has not particularly noticed you yet.
- **Seeker** - Reached at 25 piety. The god has taken note of how you live. Your first small, steady blessing begins.
- **Devoted** - Reached at 50 piety. A stable, real relationship. Your main everyday blessing settles in, and you have access to more of the god's favor in the right moments.
- **Champion** - Reached at 85 piety, the top of the bar. The rarest and strongest tier. This is full recognition, and it is meant to feel exceptional. Many ordinary playthroughs may never reach it, and that is by design.

Reaching a higher tier always keeps what the lower tiers gave you. A Champion
still carries everything the Seeker and Devoted relationship granted, plus the
Champion layer on top.

## How You Earn Piety

Piety comes from several sources at once. You do not have to chase all of
them. Living your character honestly is usually enough.

- **Everyday deeds your god likes.** The ordinary things you do that line up with a god's domain. These are the bread and butter of devotion, and they are the main reason the daily cap exists.
- **Bigger curated moments.** Certain quests, shrine visits, defending the faithful, putting down a great threat, completing a meaningful rite. These weigh more heavily than a routine deed because they clearly show the god who you are.
- **Occasional one-off devotional acts.** Specific, deliberate acts of worship that come up now and then, fitting to the god or to your people.
- **Favors.** In a fitting moment, a god may grant you a temporary blessing - a "favor." More on these below; they are help, not a piety source you control.
- **Your people's ever-present layer.** Some races carry a quiet, always-on background of faith tied to who they are. A Dunmer's ancestors are always near. A Khajiit lives inside the Lunar Lattice from birth. An Argonian's bond to the Hist is part of their very being. For those races, this layer hums along underneath everything else. Your race guide explains whether your people have one.

**The single most important rule of earning piety: variety beats grinding.**
Repeating the exact same deed earns less and less each time you do it. Killing
the same kind of enemy over and over, or hammering the same shrine, will dry
up fast. A god is not impressed by a loop. A god is impressed by a life. Mix
your devotional acts, and your piety will climb far more smoothly than if you
try to farm one thing.

## How You Lose Piety

Faith can fade or be spent, but the mod is built so that you do not casually
crumble.

- **Deeds your god dislikes.** Acting against a god's values costs you with that god. A god of mercy frowns on needless slaughter. A god of the dead turns away from necromancy. These are real losses, but they are tied to meaningful acts, not to ordinary play.
- **Neglect and natural drift.** If you simply stop paying attention to a god for a long stretch, the relationship slowly cools on its own. This is gentle drift, not a slap. It is covered more fully in "When a God Goes Quiet" below.
- **Your tiers are protected.** Reaching a tier is an achievement, and the mod treats it like one. Once you have earned Seeker, Devoted, or Champion standing with a god, you do not tumble back down it over a couple of missed days. The relationship can weaken if you truly abandon it, but the floors beneath your earned tiers hold. You will not lose hard-won Champion standing to a quiet week.
- **The broad-worship cap.** If you spread your faith across several gods at once rather than committing to one, you are capped at Devoted. This is not a penalty so much as a fact of breadth: you can be a fine, well-rounded worshipper of many gods and reach a stable Devoted standing, but you cannot become any single god's Champion that way. Champion belongs to commitment.

## Patrons and Commitment

This is the choice at the heart of the mod: do you honor many gods, or commit
to one?

- **Honoring many gods (broad worship).** This is a complete and culturally normal way to play for the races whose traditions allow it. You keep a rounded relationship with a whole pantheon, you get a blended set of blessings, and you cap at Devoted. Nothing about this is a failure state. It is a valid devotional life - "acknowledged by the gods, beloved by none specifically."
- **Committing to one god.** When you have built enough standing and a god has seen enough of who you are, that god may come to claim you. This commitment is what unlocks the road to Champion. A focused patron gives you that god's full, sharper blessings rather than the softer blended ones, and only a focused patron can ever reach the top of the bar.

**How a god comes to claim you** depends on your race, and the details live in
your race guide. For most races, it works like this: you worship broadly, you
live your character, and at some point a god whose domain matches how you have
been playing makes you an offer. You can accept, and commit - or you can say
"not yet," which costs you nothing and lets you keep worshipping broadly while
you wait to see who else takes notice. You do not pick your patron from a menu.
You live, and the right god finds you.

A couple of races work differently. Khajiit, for example, never get a formal
offer at all - their patron emerges silently from how they live. Others choose
a path or a tradition up front. Your race guide spells out which kind you are.

Once you accept a patron, that commitment is stable. The god is yours and you
are theirs; other gods stop trying to claim you. Neglect can strain the bond
over time, but it will not silently swap your patron out from under you.

## Rewards Explained

Devotion's rewards come in three layers. They stack: a Champion of a god is
enjoying all three at once.

Note: these are current beta values and may be tuned before release.

**1. An always-on blessing that grows with each tier.** This is your
dependable, passive sign that devotion matters. It is modest by design - the
mod is meant to enrich your character's life, not to outclass perk overhauls
or major combat mods - but it grows as you climb. As an example, a Nord who
commits to Kyne, the storm-mother, carries her weather in his lungs as a
Devoted (Fortify Stamina +25, Frost Resistance +10%), and at Champion the wind
is genuinely at his back (Fortify Stamina +40, Frost Resistance +25%). Every
god's blessing fits that god's domain - a Mara worshipper mends and heals, a
Stendarr worshipper guards and wards, a Zenithar worshipper carries more and
trades better. Each blessing stays modest enough to sit comfortably alongside
a large mod list. The exact effects differ by god and race; your race guide
gives the specifics for your people.

Note that a patron's own blessing begins at the Devoted step. Before you commit
to a god, the blessing you carry is your people's broad one - the Old Ways for a
Nord, the Green Way or a Breton tradition, the Lunar Lattice for a Khajiit - and
that one does start at Seeker.

Devotion states its bonuses as flat pools rather than percentage regeneration
rates, so they stay meaningful under overhauls like Requiem that switch off
passive regeneration entirely.

**2. The one-at-a-time "favor."** A favor is a stronger, temporary blessing a
god grants in a fitting moment - when the right religious context is truly
present. It might steady you for a single hard fight, smile on a journey,
bless a death-rite, or carry you through a sacred place. Favors are automatic.
You never put them on a hotbar or cast them; the god gives them when the
moment is right, and they fade when the moment passes. Some are brief and
quiet (you simply feel the effect), and some are rarer and clearly announced.

The key limit: **only one favor can be active at a time, across all gods.**
You will never have two divine boosts stacked together. While one favor is
active, another cannot start. Once it expires, the next fitting moment can
bring a new one. This keeps a favor feeling like a moment of grace rather than
a pile of buffs.

**3. Recognition.** The least mechanical and often the most satisfying layer.
As you rise, the religious and cultural world begins to treat you differently:
NPCs and dialogue acknowledge what you are, certain places and interactions
open to you, and your standing earns access that a stranger to the faith never
gets. Recognition is about being known. It is the part of the Champion tier
that should feel like the god noticed who you were before you knew it mattered.

## Champion Special Powers

Champion is the top of the bar, and for most gods it is a strong passive
blessing - its "special" is the recognition itself. But a few Champions carry a
scripted power on top of the stats, almost always a once-a-day near-death save.
There is at most **one such save per race**, and it is the rarest, most dramatic
thing devotion can do for you. Each one below is verified against the shipped
records; every other Champion tier is passive.

| Race | God | Power | What it does |
|------|-----|-------|--------------|
| Khajiit | Baan Dar | Baan Dar Remembers | Once per day, when a blow would drop you (health falls to a fifth or lower), Baan Dar slips you out of death's hand - you are put back on your feet instead of dying. Khajiit's single cheat-death. |
| Nord | Shor | Shor Remembers | Once per day, a killing blow becomes a last stand - Shor pulls you back from the edge and you survive it. Nord's single cheat-death. |
| Redguard | HoonDing | HoonDing Makes Way | Once per day, a lethal blow is refused - HoonDing opens the road back from the edge and you keep your feet. Redguard's single cheat-death. |
| Redguard | Tu'whacca | Death-rite restoration | From Devoted upward, keeping a death-rite - putting the restless dead to rest, or carrying a Far Shores token - restores a flat measure of health once a day: about 30 at Devoted, 50 at Champion. A flat restore, so it is felt even under Requiem. A heal, not a cheat-death, so it does not count as the race's save. |
| Dunmer | The Ancestors | The Ancestors Watch | Armed by keeping the home prayer (pray at your ancestral urn or hearth). For that day, if you are brought to the brink, once the ancestors pull you back. Disarmed at dawn, so it is re-earned daily. Dunmer's single cheat-death. |
| Bosmer | Baan Dar (Bandit Road) | Baan Dar Opens the Gap | On the Bandit Road, once a day a killing blow becomes an opening - you slip it and break away. Bosmer's single cheat-death. |
| Argonian | Sithis (the Void) | Void near-death surge | While the Void is your active focus, once a day near death the Nothing lends a brief surge - a burst of stamina and momentum to fight your way clear. |
| Orc | Malacath (any focus) | The Code Holds | With Malacath's regard, once in a fight when near death the Code holds - a flat burst of health (more at Devoted and up) keeps you standing. |

Altmer, Breton, and Imperial have no scripted power: every one of their Champion
tiers is a strong passive. A cheat-death fires at most once a day (the Orc's
holds once per fight) and only when your health has already dropped to a fifth or
less - it will not save you from a single overwhelming hit that skips that window.
Your race guide lists the exact per-tier blessings for your people.

## When a God Goes Quiet (Neglect)

If you drift away from a god - stop doing the things that god cares about for a
good while - the relationship goes quiet. This is neglect.

The important thing to understand is that neglect is the **absence of
attention, not a punishment.** A neglected god does not curse you or hunt you
down. Mostly, the small graces simply stop. The blessing that used to feel
present feels distant. The little confirmations that the god was watching fade
out. For a Nord drifting from Kyne, the weather just stops feeling like it is
on your side - it becomes ordinary weather again. For a Dunmer, the ash-prayer
goes quiet; the ancestors do not respond. It feels like a relationship cooling,
not a wound.

And it lifts when you return. Neglect is not a trap. Start honoring the god
again and the relationship warms back up. (One race, the orthodox Bosmer Old
Contract path, has a deliberate exception where abandonment can become
permanent - that is unique to that path and your race guide will warn you. For
everyone else, you can always come back.)

## Curses (Vampirism and Lycanthropy)

Becoming undead or a beast changes your standing with the gods, sometimes
profoundly. Vampirism and lycanthropy are not just gameplay states in Devotion
- they are spiritual conditions that strain or break faith.

In general terms: a curse can close certain gods to you, sour relationships
that were warm, or shift how a god reads everything you do. Becoming a vampire,
in particular, tends to cut hard against gods tied to mortality, the honored
dead, and the natural afterlife.

But the consequences vary sharply by race, because each people's theology
meets the curse differently. For one race a curse is total collapse with a
long road back; for another it is a serious strain that can be carried and
recovered from; for a few it is genuinely complicated rather than simply bad.
Curing the curse usually begins a path back to your gods, though some races
carry a lasting scar from what happened. **What a curse means for you depends
entirely on your race - see your race guide for the specifics.**

## Reading Your Standing In Game

You are never left guessing where you stand. The mod shows you in several
places:

- **The Devotion panel.** Your main window into your religious life. It has a few views: *Patron* (who your gods are, your standing with them, and your committed patron if you have one), *Today* (the devotional acts you have done and how the day is shaping your faith), and *Book of Days* (a running journal of your devotional life over time, so you can look back on the arc of your faith rather than just the present moment).
- **The MCM menu.** The mod's settings and status menu, where you can check details and adjust options.
- **Notification toasts.** Short messages that pop up at meaningful moments - a tier reached, a favor granted, a god going quiet, a curse straining your faith. These are how the mod taps you on the shoulder during play without pulling you out of the game.

Between these, you can always see who is watching you, how close you are to
them, and what your devotion has earned. For everything specific to your
people - which gods, which deeds, how commitment works, and what your curses
mean - turn to your race guide.
