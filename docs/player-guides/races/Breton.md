# Breton - The Tradition You Walk

## Overview

Bretons are a contradiction, and they have made peace with it. Half-elven by blood, they built an identity as the most human of the human races, yet they carry both the logic of the Divines and the old magical traditions of the elves at the same time. A Breton can keep a knight's vow and consult a witch in the same week and find nothing strange about it. For a Breton, mixing faiths is not a flaw. It is the faith.

Because of that, your faith is organized less around a single god and more around a tradition you choose to live. The gods give your tradition shape, but the tradition comes first. You pick one of three at the very start, and that choice shapes the rest of your playthrough.

The three traditions barely overlap. The Knight's Road is civic honor and protective justice under the Divines. The Hidden Art is secret occult study, with one foot in the Daedric world and a public face to protect. The Green Way is druidic covenant with the old nature-god Y'ffre. Each comes with its own gods, its own way of earning standing, and its own hidden measure that watches how you carry yourself. (For the basics of piety, tiers, and patrons, see the How Devotion Works primer.)

## Your Gods

Your gods depend on the tradition you walk.

Knight's Road gods (the Divines and their kin):

- Stendarr - god of mercy, justice, and righteous restraint. The Knight's Road reads him most sharply.
- Akatosh - the Dragon God of time and order; patron of dragonslayers and the keeping of oaths.
- Mara - goddess of love, family, and community.
- Arkay - keeper of the cycle of life and death; enemy of the undead.
- Julianos, Zenithar, Kynareth, and Dibella can all receive Knight's Road devotion as well.

Hidden Art gods (occult and arcane):

- Magnus - the elder mage-god. Forbidden study is his above all, and he is the god who answers the Hidden Art most directly.
- Julianos - god of wisdom and disciplined study; the lawful, restrained face of the secret art.
- The Daedric Princes most legible to a Breton witch reach you through the wider Daedric system: Hermaeus Mora (forbidden knowledge), Hircine (the hunt), Namira (corruption and the discarded), and Nocturnal (shadow and the Nightingales). These yield piety, not punishment, for a Breton on this road.

Green Way gods (the old nature covenant):

- Y'ffre - the Storyteller, the first and oldest god of the forest. The heart of the Green Way, and the god who answers your covenant work.
- Kynareth - goddess of the sky and the wild; she honors the open air and the gathered herb.
- Magnus - the elder mage-god, tied to Alteration and rare arcane lore.
- Phynaster - an elven ancestor-hero, honored through appreciation of elven heritage and craft.

Foreign gods and the Daedric Princes are not approached freely. Normal patron offers come only from the tradition you chose. There is no casual god-hopping.

## Getting Started

At the very start you must choose your tradition. There is no default and no fallback - the game will keep asking until you pick. This is the single most important choice you make as a Breton, because tradition switching is not available in normal play. Choose deliberately.

The three branches:

- The Knight's Road. You serve the Divines through honor, mercy, and protection. You gain standing by clearing the unnatural, healing the hurt, and answering for your own crimes. Your hidden measure here is your Knightly Vow - your honor. It begins full and is cut down when you take expedient, dishonorable shortcuts. This is the hardest tradition to keep, because Skyrim constantly offers you easy corruption.

- The Hidden Art. You study the occult in secret. You gain standing through forbidden books, master spells, and patient arcane work. Your hidden measure is your Exposure - how visible your witchcraft has become. It starts at nothing. The whole tension of this path is whether you stay hidden and quiet, or let your practice show and take both the reward and the cost of a blown cover.

- The Green Way. You keep a druidic covenant with Y'ffre and live close to the land. You gain standing by sleeping under the open sky, gathering what the wild offers, and hunting with respect. Your hidden measure is your Druidic Standing - the covenant's read on you. It starts at a neutral middle, grows with covenant work, and quietly frays if you spend all your time in cities and dungeons. This path also has one hard interaction with the werewolf curse (see If You Are Cursed).

Only the measure tied to your own tradition moves. A knight's occult curiosity does not raise a witch's Exposure, and a witch's forest walks do not build a druid's standing.

## How You Gain Piety

Remember that piety is tracked separately for each god, daily gain is capped at about 4.3 per god per day, and repeating the same deed earns less each time. Variety matters far more than grinding.

Knight's Road deeds:

- Kill the undead and the Daedra. Stendarr, Arkay, Akatosh, and Y'ffre all approve of clearing the unnatural, and Kynareth joins them against the Daedra.
- Heal or cure someone in need. Mara reads this most warmly; Stendarr, Arkay, Kynareth, and Akatosh all take note.
- Pay off a bounty by serving your time. The merciful gods read a debt served as a debt made right, and Stendarr and Mara both credit it.
- Study and learn for Julianos: skill books, spell tomes, lore, Words of Power, enchanting, and brewing.
- Show mercy or shield the helpless where a quest turns on it. Several quests are scored on exactly that choice - see Quests That Move Your Standing.
- Two quests are the vow itself. Seeing the Book of Love through to its end, and laying the dead of Morthal to rest, both renew your Knightly Vow in full and please Stendarr.

Hidden Art deeds:

- Read the forbidden texts. Three exist: Herbane's Bestiary: Hagravens, The Madmen of the Reach, and a witch's note left out in the wilds. Each one deepens your practice, pleases Magnus, and pushes your Exposure up.
- Learn a master spell of the forbidden schools. Dead Thrall and Conjure Dremora Lord both count, and both carry the same weight as a forbidden book.
- Keep up the quiet work. Spell tomes, lore books, and enchanting all count as ordinary practice - they feed Julianos through his love of study, and they nudge your Exposure up a little at a time.
- Serve a Daedric Prince. Daedric quests pay you through your dealings with the Prince himself, in the same way any Daedric worship does. On this road that is piety rather than sin.

Green Way deeds:

- Gather what the covenant asks for. Four harvests carry the Green Way: spriggan sap, taproot, nirnroot, and red nirnroot. These raise your Druidic Standing and please Y'ffre. Ordinary plant-picking is not the same thing, though Kynareth still credits it in a small way.
- Sleep under the open sky rather than in an inn or a house. Y'ffre, Kynareth, and Akatosh all honor it, and it counts as covenant practice.
- Hunt wild game, and cook what you take. The Green Pact takes meat with respect and honors the slain, so both the hunt and the meal earn Y'ffre's favor.
- See the Eldergleam through. Restoring the great tree is covenant work of the first order.
- Read the old tales of the wild where you find them. They are rare, but they count.

To reach the top tier you must commit to a single god within your tradition. Honoring several at once is welcome, but it holds you at Devoted.

## How You Lose Piety

Each god dislikes things, and doing them costs you piety with that god:

- The protective gods - Stendarr, Mara, Arkay, Julianos, Kynareth, Akatosh, and Y'ffre - all turn away from murdering the defenseless and assaulting the innocent.
- Stendarr, Arkay, Kynareth, Y'ffre, Mara, Akatosh, and Magnus punish raising the undead. Necromancy is among the worst offenses to them.
- Stendarr, Mara, and Julianos dislike theft and trespass.
- Stendarr, Arkay, Kynareth, Magnus, and Akatosh dislike accepting Daedric artifacts; that bargain betrays the Vigil and honest work. Note that on the Hidden Art road the same act still pays you through the Prince - this is the Divines' half of the ledger, and it bites hardest on the Knight's Road.

Beyond dislikes, a few quieter forces erode your standing:

- Neglect. If more than five days pass without a single act your tradition recognizes, the tradition grows distant: Maximum Health -10 and Magic Resistance -5% until you return to it. It is a nudge, not a punishment, and any act of your own practice clears it.
- Natural drift. Standing settles over time if you stop tending it.
- The broad-worship cap. Honoring several gods of your tradition is fine, but it holds you at Devoted. Reaching Champion requires committing to one.

Your hidden measure is the big one, and it differs by tradition:

- Knightly Vow (Knight's Road). Your honor. It begins full. Murdering the defenseless, assaulting the innocent, stealing, and treacherous murder in a quest all cut into it. Honorable work - healing, curing, clearing the undead and the Daedra, serving your time - restores it in full. Let it fall far enough and the vow strains: Speech -5 and Restoration -5 until you make it right. Let it fall further and the Road casts you out entirely: Maximum Health -15 until you earn your way back.

- Exposure (Hidden Art). How visible your witchcraft is. Every occult act raises it, and it fades by a point each day, so a quiet season can bring you back down. Let it reach the top and your cover is blown: Conjuration -8 and Illusion -8, the art turning against the hand that hides it. But a witch with nothing left to hide is a witch the Princes can finally use - at full Exposure, the Daedric Princes of the Hidden Art pay you a quarter again as much. That is the bargain of this path, and you are meant to feel both halves of it.

- Druidic Standing (Green Way). The covenant's read on you. It begins in the neutral middle, rises with covenant work, and frays a little each day if you stop. Raising the undead and defiling the wild drive it down hard. It is a measure rather than a lock: low standing does not take your blessings away. What takes your blessings away is the beast-blood - see If You Are Cursed.

## Bonuses by Tier

Your tradition is your blessing, and it arrives in two halves. **Seeker** (25 piety) and **Devoted** (50) come from practicing your tradition broadly - honoring any of its gods advances the same family. **Champion** (85) is different: it comes only when one god of your tradition reaches Champion and stands as your patron, and that god brings a capstone of their own. Only your chosen tradition's family pays; the other two stay dormant, and only one temporary favor blessing is ever active at a time.

The three traditions grow their own way through Seeker and Devoted:

- **Knight's Road** - the vow and the shield - blocking and a ward against magic.
- **Hidden Art** - the discreet mage's craft - Conjuration and Illusion.
- **Green Way** - the wild covenant - stamina and Restoration.

**Champion belongs to your patron.** Whichever god of your tradition you carry to the top brings their own capstone: Stendarr crowns the Knight's Road, Y'ffre the Green Way, and a Daedric Prince the Hidden Art - those three replace your Devoted blessing. Any other eligible god (Mara, Arkay, Akatosh, Julianos, Kynareth, Dibella, Zenithar, Talos, or Magnus) grants a boon that sits beside your Devoted blessing, so you carry both. These capstones are shared copies of the same gods' Imperial and Altmer boons.

The Bretons have **no scripted Champion power** - every capstone is a strong passive, and the reward is the recognition itself. For the exact per-patron capstone values, see the Blessings and Penalties reference.

## Unique Mechanics

The Breton's whole identity is the three traditions and the hidden measure each one keeps on you.

The first thing that makes a Breton feel different is that one starting choice forks your entire faith into three barely-overlapping playstyles - a holy knight, a secret witch, or a forest druid - and you live with it. There is no fallback and no easy switch.

The second is that something is keeping score behind the standard piety bar: your honor as a knight, your exposure as a witch, or the covenant's standing as a druid. These measures rarely pay a bonus of their own. What they do is decide what your practice costs you.

A knight who lets his honor fall finds his vow strained, and then finds himself cast out. A witch whose cover breaks finds her art turning against her hands - and finds the Princes suddenly very interested in her. A druid who takes the beast-blood finds the covenant closed until the blood is cleaned out. This is the heart of playing a Breton: you manage your public face alongside your actual practice.

## If You Are Cursed (Vampire or Werewolf)

What happens to your faith depends on your tradition, and the werewolf curse on the Green Way bites hardest of all.

Vampirism follows the ordinary rules of the curse, but each tradition reads it differently:

- Knight's Road: a horror. The Divines are lost to you and your knightly oaths are broken, with no positive substitute. This is the worst outcome for a cursed knight.
- Hidden Art: a partial home. The Volkihar court and the witch-mothers accept you, and a Daedric patron near Molag Bal remains reachable.
- Green Way: a wound. The covenant was made with the living land, and you are no longer quite among the living.

Werewolf:

- Knight's Road: theologically homeless. There is no framework for it, and you carry the social cost of the beast without any faith to set against it.
- Hidden Art: a natural fit. Hircine already lives in this frame, the Glenmoril witches are practically family, and your practice costs you nothing.
- Green Way: the hard one. The moment you first transform, the covenant closes. Your Druidic Standing remains, but the Green Way's blessings switch off while the beast-blood is in you - the druids are split on werewolves, and the covenant you swore is not. Curing your lycanthropy restores the covenant and your blessings with it. If you mean to run the Green Way and the beast together, understand that you cannot.

## Quick Reference

- Gods: Knight's Road - Stendarr, Akatosh, Mara, Arkay (and Julianos, Zenithar, Kynareth, Dibella). Hidden Art - Magnus and Julianos, plus the Daedric Princes (Hermaeus Mora, Hircine, Namira, Nocturnal). Green Way - Y'ffre, Kynareth, Magnus, Phynaster.
- Starting choice: pick one tradition - Knight's Road, Hidden Art, or Green Way. Required, no fallback, and locked for the playthrough.
- Top ways to gain: (Knight's Road) put down the undead and the Daedra, heal and cure the hurt, serve your bounty, study for Julianos. (Hidden Art) read the three forbidden texts, learn the master spells, keep up the quiet study, serve a Prince. (Green Way) gather the covenant's four harvests, sleep under the open sky, hunt and cook with respect.
- Main ways to lose: harming the innocent and raising the undead; theft and Daedric bargains (for the Divines); letting five days pass without practicing your tradition; honoring several gods at once, which holds you at Devoted; and your hidden measure - a fallen vow, a blown cover, or the beast-blood - costing you your standing or your blessings.
- Rough days to Champion: about 30-45 days of normal play, nearer 20 if you focus on one god from the start. The Knight's Road is the hardest Champion to keep, because corruption is always on offer.
