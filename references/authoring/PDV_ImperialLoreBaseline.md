# PDV Imperial Lore Baseline

**Status: LIVING**
**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Purpose:** Sourced Imperial lore baseline (Wave 1 rebaseline) for the civic-lane frame and
future copy work. Lore claims cite the in-game text they rest on. Claims that rest only on
out-of-game material are flagged as such. This race surveyed CLEAN (manifest, guard, spec,
code agree 1:1), so this is the light-pass dossier: its job is the 4E 201 era anchor, the
pantheon citations, and confirming the shipped frame. It decides nothing; owner rulings land
in the final section. It is consistent with the locked Concordat rulings in
PDV_DecisionMemo_ImperialComplianceLane.md, which are settled and not reopened here.

**Source-tier legend:**
- IN-GAME: readable book, spoken dialogue, or environment inside a shipped TES game
  (Morrowind, Oblivion, Skyrim, ESO, Blades). Game and era tagged where it matters.
- FLAVOR: in-game item/loading-screen/codex description text (canon but thin).
- OOG: official out-of-game material (Pocket Guide print editions, developer Q&A). Flagged
  wherever load-bearing.

**Era key:** Skyrim is 4E 201. The Concordat is 4E 175 -- twenty-six years before the game,
living memory for every adult. Morrowind (3E 427) and Oblivion (3E 433) describe the intact
pre-war religion; ESO is 2E, roughly 700 years earlier, and is used here only for antiquarian
color. A 3E source describes long-standing cult practice; only 4E sources describe the legal
reality the mod plays inside.

## 1. State of the faith in 4E 201

**The long-standing practice (3E and earlier).** Imperial religion is the Nine Divines, "the
major religious cult of Cyrodiil and its provinces" (Varieties of Faith in the Empire --
in-game in MW/OB/SR, a 3E-composed survey still in print at 4E 201). It was founded as an
act of statecraft: Empress Alessia, needing both her Nordic allies and her elf-acculturated
subjects, "instituted a new religion: the Eight Divines, an elegant, well-researched
synthesis of both pantheons, Nordic and Aldmeri" (Shezarr and the Divines, Faustillus
Junius -- in-game OB/SR/ON). Talos was added as the Ninth after Tiber Septim's death in
3E 38 ("it is believed he ascended to godhood and became the ninth aspect of the Nine
Divines" -- Brief History of the Empire; The Talos Mistake recounts the same ascent from
the other side). The religion is institutional to its bones: in Cyrodiil it is simply "the
Church" or "the Chapel," organized into parishes each dedicated to a Divine, with Great
Chapels, a Council of the Nine, venerated saints, and chapel altars that refuse blessing to
the infamous until they repent and pay fines "unto the Emperor" (Ten Commands: Nine
Divines -- in-game OB, 3E chapel doctrine; UESP: Lore:Imperial Cult). Abroad, the faith
travels with the Empire as the Imperial Cult, "the missionary arm of the great faiths,"
serving expatriates and garrisons from shrines in Legion forts -- doctrine: "Our Emperor is
the Defender of the Faith, and the Empire is the worldly working of the Divine Plan" (For
my Gods and Emperor -- in-game MW, 3E 427). That handbook is the direct 3E model for an
Imperial practicing outside Cyrodiil, and its title is the civic thesis whole: gods and
Emperor in one breath.

**What changed (4E).** Three ruptures separate that intact religion from 4E 201:

1. **The Covenant mechanism ended (3E 433).** The Amulet of Kings and the Dragonfires --
   Akatosh's token that "so long as your blood and oath hold true, yet so shall my blood
   and oath be true to you" (Trials of St. Alessia -- in-game OB/SR) -- were consumed
   ending the Oblivion Crisis (IN-GAME: Oblivion, Light the Dragonfires). At 4E 201 the
   Covenant survives as doctrine and identity, not as a live ritual: the Mede emperors are
   not Dragonborn. Akatosh remains "the ultimate God of the Cyrodilic Empire, where he
   embodies the qualities of endurance, invincibility, and everlasting legitimacy"
   (Varieties of Faith in the Empire) -- but the legitimacy is now carried by institutions,
   which is precisely the mod's civic register.
2. **The Great War and the Concordat (4E 171-175).** The Empire fought the Aldmeri Dominion
   to exhaustion, retook the Imperial City, and signed the White-Gold Concordat in 4E 175:
   Talos worship outlawed, the Thalmor granted the right to enforce the ban inside the
   Empire, southern Hammerfell ceded, the Blades disbanded (The Great War, Legate Justianus
   Quintius -- in-game SR). The same book records the internal Imperial split: critics held
   the treaty unnecessary; defenders held the armies "in no condition to continue" (The
   Great War; corroborated by Hadvar's and Delphine's dialogue -- in-game SR 4E 201).
3. **Markarth hardened the ban (4E 176).** Enforcement was initially loose -- "Imperial
   leniency in enforcement" (The Bear of Markarth -- in-game SR) -- until Ulfric's militia
   retook Markarth in exchange for open Talos worship, "in full violation of the White-Gold
   Concordat" (The Bear of Markarth). The Dominion's answer was justiciars: "the Empire was
   forced to maintain the White-Gold Concordat by cracking down on the worship of Talos in
   Skyrim," and by 4E 201 the Thalmor run "a violent campaign of persecution against Talos
   worshippers" from their embassy (Alvor's dialogue; Thalmor Dossier: Ulfric Stormcloak --
   both in-game SR).

**What an Imperial in Skyrim lives inside at 4E 201.** The public religion is the Eight.
The Solitude Temple of the Divines -- the faith's provincial seat, in the Imperial capital
of Skyrim -- has "eight shrines at the far end in individual alcoves with an empty alcove
where the shrine of Talos stood" (IN-GAME: Skyrim, Temple of the Divines environment). The
official line is supplied by the Empire's own liaison to the Dominion: "the Empire admits
it was wrong. The Talos Mistake will not be repeated" (The Talos Mistake, Leonora Venatus
-- in-game SR, Concordat-era propaganda; its argument is that Talos-worship dishonored both
the man Tiber Septim and "the Eight Divines, the true gods"). Around that official line,
everything is contested: Nords preach the Ninth in the open street (Heimskr's sermon --
in-game SR), rebel pamphlets cry "How can man set aside a god?" (Nords Arise! -- in-game
SR), the jarls were "financially compensated" for swallowing the terms (Balgruuf/Proventus/
Irileth conversation -- in-game SR), and the ban is named as a chief cause of the civil war
(Ulfric's dialogue; Skyrim's Rule). An Imperial expatriate carries a unique tension inside
that noise: the banned god is not a foreign folk hero to them but the FOUNDER OF THEIR OWN
STATE -- Tiber Septim, "founder and patron of the Empire" (For my Gods and Emperor) -- and
the government enforcing the ban is their own. Complying is civic loyalty and theological
amputation at once; defying is keeping faith with the Empire's own god against the Empire's
own law. No other race's religion has that shape, and it is the shape the mod ships.

**Era-check summary.** Long-standing (3E, still true at 4E 201 unless noted): the Nine as
one civic pantheon, the chapel/temple institutional style, the Imperial Cult expatriate
model, saint veneration, Ten Commands ethics. New legal reality (4E 175+, overriding 3E
practice): public roster is Eight; Talos rites are criminal; justiciars enforce. A 3E
citation below establishes practice; it never establishes legality at 4E 201.

## 2. Pantheon

The backbone source is Varieties of Faith in the Empire (Brother Mikhael Karkuxor), whose
CYRODIIL roster is instructive in itself: it lists not nine entries but twelve -- the Eight,
plus Shezarr, Tiber Septim, Morihaus, and Reman (Varieties of Faith in the Empire). The
civic Nine are a legal-liturgical frame laid over a wider field of hero-cults and saints
("the Imperial City of Cyrodiil alone boasts a vast host of saints and holy spirits" --
same source). Worship-practice citations below lean on Ten Commands: Nine Divines (in-game
OB, 3E chapel doctrine -- each Divine's command is the cleanest per-god statement of what
practice looks like).

**Akatosh (Dragon God of Time)** -- Chief deity of the Nine, "the ultimate God of the
Cyrodilic Empire ... endurance, invincibility, and everlasting legitimacy" (Varieties of
Faith in the Empire). The Covenant god: drew blood from his own heart for Alessia, "so long
as Alessia's generations were true to the dragon blood, Akatosh would endeavor to seal
tight the Gates of Oblivion" (The Amulet of Kings -- in-game OB/SR). Practice: "Serve and
obey your Emperor. Study the Covenants. Worship the Nine, do your duty" (Ten Commands) --
Akatosh's own command is civic obedience, which is why he anchors the mod's order lane.

**Talos (Tiber Septim, the Ninth)** -- "The most important hero-god of Mankind" (Varieties
of Faith in the Empire), the man who conquered Tamriel, founded the Third Empire, and
ascended -- "the man who was so loved in life became worshipped in death" (The Talos
Mistake). Practice, pre-ban: "Be strong for war. Be bold against enemies and evil, and
defend the people of Tamriel" (Ten Commands); amulets of Talos; a Great Chapel at Bruma
(UESP: Lore:Imperial Cult). At 4E 201 his worship is outlawed by the Concordat and hunted
by justiciars (The Great War; Alvor's dialogue -- in-game SR); his Skyrim practice is
hidden shrines and private amulets. The Dominion's theological claim is "that a man
couldn't achieve apotheosis" (The Talos Mistake). He is the Empire's own god banned by the
Empire's own hand -- the mod's Concordat track is the mechanical form of exactly that.

**Mara (Mother-Goddess)** -- "Nearly universal goddess ... In the Empire, she is
Mother-Goddess" (Varieties of Faith in the Empire). Practice: "Live soberly and peacefully.
Honor your parents, and preserve the peace and security of home and family" (Ten Commands);
at 4E 201 her Riften temple runs the marriage rite (IN-GAME: Skyrim, The Bonds of
Matrimony). Domain: love, family, community restoration.

**Dibella (Goddess of Beauty)** -- "In Cyrodiil, she has nearly a dozen different cults,
some devoted to women, some to artists and aesthetics, and others to erotic instruction"
(Varieties of Faith in the Empire). Practice: "Open your heart to the noble secrets of art
and love" (Ten Commands); at 4E 201 her Markarth temple keeps the Sybil tradition
(IN-GAME: Skyrim, The Heart of Dibella).

**Arkay (God of the Cycle of Life and Death)** -- "God of burials and funeral rites ...
His priests are staunch opponents of necromancy and all forms of the undead"; "sometimes
called the Mortals' God" because he did not exist before creation (Varieties of Faith in
the Empire). Practice: "Honor the earth, its creatures, and the spirits, living and dead
... do not profane the spirits of the dead" (Ten Commands); at 4E 201 every major hold's
Hall of the Dead is his (IN-GAME: Skyrim). The mod's cleanest signal family, and the
sources support that weight.

**Zenithar (God of Work and Commerce)** -- "In the Empire ... a far more cultivated god of
merchants and middle nobility. His worshippers say ... Zenithar is the god 'that will
always win'" (Varieties of Faith in the Empire). Practice: "Work hard, and you will be
rewarded. Spend wisely ... Never steal, or you will be punished" (Ten Commands).

**Stendarr (God of Mercy)** -- "Evolved from his Nordic origins into a deity of compassion
or, sometimes, righteous rule. He is said to have accompanied Tiber Septim in his later
years" (Varieties of Faith in the Empire). Practice: "Be kind and generous to the people of
Tamriel. Protect the weak, heal the sick, and give to the needy" (Ten Commands); at 4E 201
the Vigilants of Stendarr hunt Daedra and abominations (IN-GAME: Skyrim). The
compassion-AND-righteous-rule doubling is load-bearing for the mod: both mercy favor and
the Enforcer mercy-dampening live inside his sourced domain.

**Kynareth (Goddess of Air)** -- "The strongest of the Sky spirits," first to agree to
Lorkhan's plan and giver of the space for creation (Varieties of Faith in the Empire).
Practice: "Use Nature's gifts wisely. Respect her power, and fear her fury" (Ten Commands);
in the 3E Church she uniquely had no Great Chapel because "the Chapel of Kynareth is the
natural world" (Avita Vesnia's dialogue -- in-game OB); at 4E 201 her Whiterun temple
tends the Gildergreen (IN-GAME: Skyrim, The Blessings of Nature).

**Julianos (God of Wisdom and Logic)** -- "The Cyrodilic god of literature, law, history,
and contradiction. Monastic orders founded by Tiber Septim and dedicated to Julianos are
the keepers of the Elder Scrolls" (Varieties of Faith in the Empire). Practice: "Know the
truth. Observe the law. When in doubt, seek wisdom from the wise" (Ten Commands).

**Shezarr (God of Man) -- texture, the absent god.** Cyrodilic version of Lorkhan: "the
spirit behind all human undertaking, especially against Aldmeri aggression ... In the
present age of racial tolerance, Shezarr is all but forgotten" (Varieties of Faith in the
Empire -- 3E statement; no 4E source revives him). "Often called their 'Missing Sibling'":
Alessia adopted a watered-down Shor into her synthesis, and the haziness of his seat is
original to the religion; one cult among the Imperial City's thousands worships him, and
the Colovian West calls him Shor outright (Shezarr and the Divines -- in-game OB/SR).
Alessia held that "freedom" was just another name for Shezarr (The Song of Pelinal, v2 --
in-game OB). See sections 5b and 6.

**St. Alessia and the saint cults -- voice, not patrons.** Alessia, "Slave Queen of
Cyrodiil," founder-figure and first Empress, was canonized at her death and her soul set in
the Amulet of Kings (Trials of St. Alessia; The Amulet of Kings; Cleansing of the Fane).
The Church venerates saints and Living Saints, and the Ten Commands opens "By the
intercession of St. Alessia" (Ten Commands; UESP: Lore:Imperial Cult). Saints are the
register Imperial piety speaks in -- intercession, canonization, commands -- not separate
worship targets. Morihaus ("First Breath of Man," Taker of the Citadel, Alessia's demigod
consort -- Varieties of Faith in the Empire; The Adabal-a) and Pelinal Whitestrake (the
possible Shezarrine -- The Song of Pelinal) are founding-myth hero cults in the same
texture field, as is Reman ("The Cyrodiil," the Second Empire's god-hero -- Varieties of
Faith in the Empire).

The mod ships all nine Divines as focusable, with the civic broad lane and the Concordat
standing track; Shezarr, Alessia, and the hero-cults are texture (sections 5-6).

## 3. The mod's lanes

**Civic broad lane (auto-started, no setup choice).** History: Alessia's synthesis religion
carried through three empires as Church, Chapel, and Imperial Cult -- a faith one is BORN
INTO as a citizen, which is why the mod starts it without a choice; the 3E Imperial Cult
handbook literally onboards expatriates into practicing abroad (For my Gods and Emperor).
Values: the Ten Commands ethic -- mercy, honest work, lawful order, proper burial, duty to
Emperor and Empire (Ten Commands; the Nine Virtues of For my Gods and Emperor). Worship
style: public, institutional, altar-and-parish; blessing is conditional on civic standing
("neither blessing nor comfort may he expect from the altars" while infamous -- Ten
Commands). PLAYER FANTASY: you are a citizen whose religion is your role -- faith practiced
as public duty in a hostile province, where the temple, the law, and the graveyard are all
one institution and the gods notice you doing your part. It should feel like the Redguard
sects' opposite: not inherited sect obligation but membership in a working civic order.

**The Concordat standing track (parallel political modifier, five states, locked design).**
History: 4E 175 treaty, 4E 176 Markarth hardening, justiciar enforcement at 4E 201 (section
1). Values: the track models where you stand on the ban -- compliance as civic loyalty,
defiance as costly conscience -- and the sources insist BOTH are authentically Imperial
positions (The Talos Mistake argues compliance sincerely; The Great War records the
critics; the Legion's own veterans split -- Great War article dialogue citations). PLAYER
FANTASY: living under a treaty your own Empire signed -- every Thalmor patrol and hidden
shrine is a theological decision inside a political one, and the same Champion can mean
opposite things at opposite standings. Per the locked memo (PDV_DecisionMemo_ImperialComplianceLane.md, LOCKED
2026-08-25 -- settled, restated for completeness only): a standing modifier, not a worship
lane; no Champion of its own; the Talos gate fails gracefully and the cost is read from
state.

**Nine focusable Divines.** Each patron sharpens one civic virtue into a personal
relationship (race sheet, focused contrast note): focused Stendarr makes mercy protective,
focused Arkay makes death-order sacred, focused Talos makes conscience against law costly
and personal. PLAYER FANTASY: the god of your own daily practice -- the burial-keeper, the
craftsman, the merciful, the defiant -- knows your name, while the civic frame you came
from keeps running underneath.

## 4. God-to-lane associations, with evidence

The Imperial mapping is unusually easy: the Nine are ONE pantheon with one civic frame, and
the mod maps gods to virtues, not to sects. Every focused lane sits squarely inside its
god's sourced domain (section 2). Gradings, Redguard convention:

**Akatosh and the civic-order lane: lore-strong.** His own command is "serve and obey your
Emperor ... do your duty" (Ten Commands); he embodies "everlasting legitimacy" (Varieties
of Faith in the Empire); the Covenant makes Imperial order itself a religious object (The
Amulet of Kings). The compliant Imperial's Champion payoff being Akatosh (per the locked
memo) is exactly where the sources put the order-god.

**Zenithar and honest commerce: lore-strong.** "Cultivated god of merchants and middle
nobility" (Varieties of Faith in the Empire); "work hard ... never steal" (Ten Commands).
The other compliant-Champion payoff; equally well-grounded.

**Arkay and burial duty: lore-strong.** Funeral rites and staunch anti-necromancy
priesthood (Varieties of Faith in the Empire); "do not profane the spirits of the dead"
(Ten Commands); Halls of the Dead at 4E 201 (IN-GAME: Skyrim).

**Stendarr and mercy under civic pressure: lore-strong.** "Compassion or, sometimes,
righteous rule" (Varieties of Faith in the Empire) -- the double domain that lets the
Enforcer state dampen him without leaving the sources.

**Talos and faithful defiance: lore-strong, and 4E-specific.** Every 4E source frames Talos
practice as costly conscience under enforcement (section 1). The mod's rule that compliance
never earns Talos favor, and that Imperial Talos answers more faintly than Nord Talos, is a
design reading of a real sourced asymmetry: in Skyrim the Ninth's living cult voice is
Nordic (Heimskr; Nords Arise!), while the Imperial relationship to him at 4E 201 is
mediated by the treaty. Defensible as shipped.

**Mara, Dibella, Julianos, Kynareth: lore-strong, low-tension.** Each focused lane reads
directly off the Varieties of Faith domain and Ten Commands practice, and each has a live
4E 201 Skyrim surface (Riften, Markarth, court wizards and the College, Whiterun).

**Concordat standing as Talos gain AND decay modifier: consistent with the sources.** The
ban operates in lore precisely as ACCESS -- shrines removed, rites criminalized, worshipers
hunted -- not as Talos changing his nature. A political track that throttles gain and
governs offers, rather than a rival buff lane, matches that shape (and is the locked
architecture: ConcordatStanding "MODIFIES Talos access/offer-priority/pressure, it is NOT a
parallel buff track" -- PDV_ImperialRewardRecords.spec.json).

## 5. Options analysis (light pass -- this race surveyed clean)

Context: no drift to adjudicate. Two frame questions were posed for confirmation; neither
proposes records.

**(a) Is the civic single-lane frame (no sects) the right Imperial shape at 4E 201? YES --
confirmed from lore.**
- The sources describe ONE unitary religion with ONE institutional hierarchy: "the Church,"
  parishes, Great Chapels, a Council of the Nine, one missionary arm (UESP: Lore:Imperial
  Cult; For my Gods and Emperor). There is no sourced sect schism inside the Nine Divines
  tradition at any point after the Alessian Order died with the War of Righteousness in the
  First Era -- nothing remotely like the Crown/Forebear split.
- The one genuine 4E division inside Imperial faith is exactly the one the mod already
  models: compliance versus conscience on the Talos ban (The Great War's critics-vs-
  defenders; The Talos Mistake versus the rebel texts). The Concordat track IS the Imperial
  sect axis, done as a continuum instead of a roster -- and unlike a sect choice, the
  sources show individuals moving along it, which a five-state track captures and a
  baseline choice would not.
- What a richer frame would look like (texture threads only, NO records proposed): a Chapel
  thread (parish piety, saint intercession, almsgiving -- the Ten Commands register), a
  Legion thread (cult shrines in Legion forts, "for my gods and Emperor" as one oath), and
  a merchant thread (Zenithar's "cultivated god of merchants and middle nobility"). All
  three are already speakable inside the civic lane's existing hook families
  (burial/mercy/lawful-order/honest-work); they are voices of one religion, not divisions
  of it. RECOMMENDATION (not a decision): keep the single civic lane; if copy ever wants
  more texture, borrow these three voices rather than adding structure.

**(b) Shezarr as texture-only: is that lore-correct? YES -- and there is one small missed
thread worth a future note.**
- Correct on the evidence: "all but forgotten" already in the 3E survey (Varieties of Faith
  in the Empire), one cult among the Imperial City's thousands (Shezarr and the Divines),
  no shrine, blessing, quest, or NPC practice anywhere in 4E 201 Skyrim to hook a signal
  to. A focusable Shezarr would out-run the sources on worship AND have the worst
  detection surface of any candidate in the mod: there is nothing in the engine to detect.
  Texture-only is both lore-correct and mechanically forced.
- The missed thread (copy, not roster): Imperial theology already contains a god-shaped
  hole -- the Missing Sibling whose absence is doctrinally acknowledged ("it both
  recognized the importance of Shezarr and emphasized his absence" -- Artorius Ponticus
  Answers Your Questions, ESO 2E, antiquarian corroboration of the OB/SR book). At 4E 201
  the Empire has cut a SECOND god-shaped hole in its own pantheon, and the Solitude temple
  displays it as an empty alcove where Talos stood (IN-GAME: Skyrim environment). The rhyme
  between the Missing Sibling and the empty alcove is real, sourced at both ends, and
  purely a wording opportunity -- e.g. civic-lane or Survey copy that lets a learned voice
  note the Empire has been down this road of absent gods before. RECOMMENDATION (not a
  decision): record the parallel as an approved copy thread in the wording backlog; build
  nothing.

## 6. Considered and rejected (non-focusable by design)

Recorded so future greps hit an explanation rather than a gap. Nothing here is a candidate
under current rulings.

- **Shezarr** -- the absent god is absent on purpose: "all but forgotten" (Varieties of
  Faith in the Empire), his seat's emptiness doctrinally acknowledged (Shezarr and the
  Divines). No 4E worship surface exists to detect. Texture: the missing-sibling motif and
  the section 5b copy thread.
- **St. Alessia and the saint cults** -- saints are the VOICE of Imperial piety
  (intercession, canonization, "by the intercession of St. Alessia" -- Ten Commands), not
  patrons beside the Nine. Alessia's own divinity is covenant-object, not cult-recipient
  (The Amulet of Kings). Available as register and copy color everywhere.
- **Morihaus and Pelinal** -- founding-myth hero cults (The Adabal-a; The Song of Pelinal);
  Varieties of Faith lists them in the Cyrodiil roster as cultural memory, not practiced
  worship, and 4E 201 Skyrim offers them no surface. Hero-cult texture only. (Reman sits in
  the same bucket.)
- **The Eight-vs-Nine public/private split** -- this is COPY guidance, not roster: the
  public register at 4E 201 says "the Eight" or "the Divines" (The Talos Mistake; the
  Solitude temple's eight shrines), and saying "the Nine" aloud is itself a position on the
  ban (Heimskr; Nords Arise!). Compliant-register surfaces should count to eight;
  defiant-register surfaces may count to nine; neutral surfaces say "the Divines." The
  roster itself ships all nine focusable regardless -- the split is in how copy COUNTS, not
  in what the player can reach.
