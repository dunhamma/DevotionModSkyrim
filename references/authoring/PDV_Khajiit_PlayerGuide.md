# Walking Under the Moons -- A Khajiit's Guide to Devotion

*A player's companion to the Khajiit path in PlayerDevotion. No menus, no numbers -- just what it
means to live faithfully under Jone and Jode.*

---

## The Lattice holds you

Khajiit do not choose a god the way a Nord chooses Talos or a Breton chooses a coven. You are born
already inside the **Lunar Lattice** -- the great pattern the moons weave, that decides what shape a
Khajiit takes and how their road runs. Riddle'Thar is not a god you pray *to* so much as the order you
pray *within*. Every Khajiit carries this. It is the quiet floor beneath everything else.

You tend the Lattice simply by living as Khajiit live: watching the moons, reading the old lunar
lore, keeping a cadence of return to the places that are yours on the road. Do this and the moons
"know your form, and the road knows your step." Neglect it and the pattern thins -- not in anger, just
in distance.

## The five who walk nearest

Above the substrate, five moon-gods can come to walk nearest your particular road. You never *declare*
one. You simply behave, over weeks, in a way that favors one of them -- and one day you notice, in the
quiet readout of your standing, that a god has drawn close. That is how Khajiit faith works: it is
**recognized, not announced.**

- **Khenarthi**, the wind-voiced, road-knowing, merciful. The easiest to walk with, because her
  worship is just the honest road: travel it on foot, aid the caravans, respect the sky. If you live
  as a wanderer and a caravaneer, Khenarthi is already at your shoulder.

- **Azurah**, threshold-knowing and twilight-voiced -- the mother who shaped the Khajiit and guards
  their passage. Hers are the in-between hours, dawn and dusk, the moments of fate and threshold. She
  is also the one who does not look away when the dark pressures take you.

- **Baan Dar**, the Bandit God, warm to the outcast, the god of pariahs and reversals. He loves the
  fight you should have lost and won anyway -- outnumbered, bloodied, clever at the last moment. Walk
  far enough with Baan Dar and his luck becomes literal: when the killing blow comes, once a day, it
  simply... turns aside. The pariah's god keeps his own on their feet.

- **Rajhin**, the Purring Liar, the greatest thief who ever lived -- performer's voice, delighted,
  legend-making. He cares nothing for petty crime or clumsy snatching. He loves the *elegant* theft:
  the impossible target, the untouchable purse, the thing taken so cleanly it becomes a story. Shadow
  sits closer to your shoulder the more of his art you practice.

- **Alkosh**, the Dragon King of Time -- rare, immense, order-keeping. His are the great kills against
  chaos: the named dragons, the words of power wrested from the wall. He is order against Lorkhaj's
  chaos, and he holds the devout "a little more firmly together."

## The moons and the month

The Lattice turns on the real moons overhead. Each of the eight phases **belongs** to one of the five
-- the full moon to Alkosh, the new moon to Rajhin, the crescents and quarters to the others. When a
phase belongs to a god you have grown close to, the moons answer your standing more readily, and a
small grace of that god settles on you for those nights: the road asks a little less of you under
Khenarthi, twilight gathers more readily under Azurah, the pariah's luck keeps your feet under you
under Baan Dar, shadow sits closer under Rajhin, order holds you together under Alkosh. It is never a
schedule to obey -- only a gift that comes around with the sky.

## When the dark gets in

Khajiit theology is gentler about the curses than most. Lycanthropy and vampirism damage your
*belonging*, not your Khajiit self, and both can be walked back.

- Take **Hircine's beast-shape** and you carry one form too many. The Lattice still holds you, but
  *strained* -- the moons are about form, and the wolf is a competing one. The caravans keep their
  distance. You are watched, not erased. Set the wolf down and the Lattice holds a single shape again.

- Take the **vampire's thirst** and the Lattice is *corrupted and thinned*. The community will fear
  you, and rightly -- but the moons do not disown their own, and Azurah, at least, will not look away.
  Cure the thirst and the corruption lifts; the moonlight was always waiting.

- Live too long in **the shadow** -- night-only, predatory, drawn to the dark between the moons -- and
  you can *drift*. This is not ordinary night-work or honest stealth; it is a whole life turned toward
  the dark. The Lattice loosens its hold, and Khenarthi's road and Azurah's twilight both feel far
  away. It is the one strain that comes from your own pattern of living, not from a bite or a curse.

## Going quiet

Stop watching the moons, stop returning home on a cadence, and devotion does not punish you -- it just
grows quiet. Standing thins, the patron who had drawn near begins to fade, the caravans forget your
face. Pick the practice back up and it returns; the moons keep no grudge. Khajiit faith is a thing you
*live*, day to day, not a debt you settle.

*Walk the road. Watch the moons. Let the god who fits your life come to you. The Lattice will hold the
rest.*
