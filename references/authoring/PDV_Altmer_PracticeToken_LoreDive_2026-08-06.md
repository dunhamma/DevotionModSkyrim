# Altmer practice token -- lore dive (2026-08-06)

**Question:** what would an Altmer actually carry and use for a daily, portable devotional practice?
**Object under review:** `0716E4 PDV_MISC_AltmerPracticeFocus`, "Ancestral Focus", currently the
placeholder vanilla mesh `Dungeons\Mines\Ore\IngotMoonstone.nif`.

> **ANSWERED 2026-08-06: the calian.** The owner surfaced an ESO object that fits better than
> anything reconstructed below, and it converges with this document's central finding rather than
> contradicting it -- see "The calian" at the end. The ranked candidates in the body are kept as the
> reasoning that led there, not as live proposals.

**Constraints from our own design, which narrow the lore search before it starts:**

- The token feeds the **deity-agnostic ancestral spine**, not a god. Its iconography must be
  *cultural/ancestral*, never Auri-El's (no sun-discs, no bow, no time imagery).
- Its whole purpose (P14) is the **indoor path** -- what you use when the outdoor dawn observance
  cannot be kept: in a jail cell, mid-dungeon, in a foreign inn.
- It must read as something a player carries permanently without it looking like crafting loot.

---

## What the lore actually says

### 1. Altmer ancestor veneration is about orderly DEPARTURE, not presence

This is the single most important finding, and it inverts the obvious design instinct.

> "Although they venerate their ancestors, they only do so by respecting the orderly and blissful
> passage of these spirits from this world to the next. That is, High Elves believe it is cruel and
> unnatural to encourage the spirits of the dead to linger in Mundus."

The wiki cites *Ancestors and the Dunmer* directly against it -- the contrast with Dunmer practice is
explicit in the source, not something we are inferring. The Dunmer keep the dead **near**: household
shrines, ancestral tombs, Ancestor Guardians summoned to aid the living. The Altmer consider exactly
that cruel.

**Therefore the Dunmer Ancestral Urn is the WRONG precedent to parallel.** An Altmer carrying a
vessel of ancestral remains, or anything that reads as housing an ancestor's presence, is not "the
Altmer version of the urn" -- it is the Dunmer heresy in Altmer hands. Our instinct to match the
Dunmer bespoke NIF with an Altmer equivalent was right about *effort* and wrong about *form*.

### 2. For an Altmer, ancestral existence IS the written record

> "For the Altmer, Xarxes records not just the life stories of individual elves, but all the
> connections of lineage and heritance that bind them together and link them to their ancestors. As
> nothing is more important to an Altmer than their ancestry..."

> "For those who die in dishonor, their families will strike all traces of their existence from
> their records, and they will not be remembered as an ancestor. They will simply vanish from
> memory, a fate worse than death itself."

And every household keeps genealogical records reaching back to the Merethic Era, before written
history.

That last quote is the key to the whole design. **To be erased from the record is worse than dying.**
An Altmer's ancestors do not persist as spirits to be housed or fed -- they persist as *names,
correctly kept*. The devotional object is therefore a **record**, and the devotional act is
**keeping it, reading it, adding to it**.

This is already how our own Xarxes lane talks: "Xarxes has written your name into the record."
The lore and the mod are pointing at the same object; the mesh simply has not caught up.

### 3. The ancestor-site is architectural and fixed -- which is exactly why a portable token exists

Altmer cremate and inter ashes in **wall shrines**. Kinlords and kings build mausoleums where
generations rest and descendants "come to present their respects and to seek guidance", so those
sites double as temples to the Ancestors, tended by priests.

None of that travels. An Altmer in Skyrim is cut off from the kinhouse wall-shrine by an ocean --
which is precisely the gap P14's token fills. The lore also leaves a small, honest opening for a
personal object:

> "Altmer do not bury their dead. Sentimental folks may erect shrines or **make something to
> remember a lost loved one by**."

Note the framing: *sentimental*. A personal keepsake is tolerated rather than doctrinal. That is a
good tone for a player item -- it is your practice, not the Kinhouse's rite.

### 4. Altmer sacred design is governed by numerology, and 2 is forbidden

- **Good numbers:** 3 (the Prime Celestials -- sun and the two moons), 5 (the Elements), 8 (the
  Planets, and 3+5), and 16 (their sum) which is "very powerful".
- **Bad number: 2.** Duality is considered *impossible* by the Altmer, is read as a metaphysical
  force of Man, and they "rarely do anything in twos aside from romantic couplings".
- The finest structures "echo the divine geometries of Aetherius", and cities are laid out on
  numerological principles **derived from ancestral records**. There is even a Garden of Sacred
  Numbers built for contemplating them.

**Design consequence:** whatever we build should be 3-, 5-, 8-, or 16-fold in its geometry, and must
**not** be a paired, twinned, hinged, or two-part object. A diptych would be actively wrong. An
octagonal or eight-sided form is the safest strong choice: 8 is both the Planets and 3+5.

### 5. Kinhouse and Calan

"Kinhouse" (also *Calan*) is the Altmeri family/clan unit. Lineage is the organising principle of
Summerset society, and status derives from it. A token that names the *kinhouse* rather than a god
sits correctly on the deity-agnostic spine.

## What this rules out

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Urn, reliquary, ash-vessel, anything holding remains | The Dunmer error. Altmer doctrine is that the dead should depart, not linger. |
| Sun-disc, Auri-El iconography, bow/shield motifs | Deity-specific; the spine is deity-agnostic by design (2026-08-04 Dawn Relocation). |
| Ancestor figurine or bust carried on the person | Edges toward housing a presence. Defensible as a fixed household object, wrong as a carried one. |
| Any two-part, hinged, or mirrored form | 2 is the Bad Number and is read as a force of Man. |
| The current moonstone ingot | Right material, wrong object: it reads as smithing loot, carries no devotional signal, and has no numerology. |

## What this rules in -- ranked

**1. A portable lineage record. (Best fit, and it is not close.)**
A rolled vellum or a thin inscribed tablet carrying the names of the line -- the household
genealogical record reduced to something a traveller can carry. The act of "practice" is reading it,
reciting it, keeping it current. It is cultural not divine, it works indoors, it needs no shrine, and
it aligns with the mod's existing Xarxes voice. Physically: moonstone, crystal, or gold-leafed
vellum; an 8-fold or 5-fold border; names in Aldmeris script.

**2. A faceted crystalline focus.**
The Aldmeri crystalline tradition is well established (Crystal Tower; the Transparent Law), and
numerology gives a natural rule for the cut -- an eight-faceted stone. Reads as contemplative rather
than looted. Weaker than option 1 only because it says "elf" rather than "ancestry", and the spine
is specifically about lineage.

**3. A name-cord or knotted tally.**
A cord knotted once per remembered generation, counted through during observance. Fits the "make
something to remember a loved one by" line and the counting numerology. **Flagged as inference** --
I found no canonical Altmer prayer-cord, so this is us building on the pattern rather than citing it.

## Practical mesh options

**Reuse (no commission):**

- `DLC01\Clutter\Animated\PortalGem01\PortalGemWhite01.nif` -- the Diamond **Paragon**. A large
  faceted gem in an ornate setting, from the Forgotten Vale, i.e. Snow Elf / Chantry material and so
  Aldmeri-descended rather than Dwemer or Nordic. The closest vanilla object to option 2. Other
  colours exist (amethyst/ruby/emerald/sapphire) if a tint helps distinguish it.
- `DLC01\Dungeons\Castle\Animated\StoneBarrierSocket\CasStoneBarrierKey01.nif` -- the "Weystone
  **Focus**". Already named a focus; worth looking at even if it is stone rather than crystal.
- A vanilla scroll mesh, for option 1 on zero budget -- with the caveat that it will read as a spell
  scroll to any player, which is a real UI-confusion cost.

**Commission (matching the Dunmer precedent `PDV\Clutter\PDV_DunmerAncestralUrn.nif`):**

A small inscribed tablet or a bound roll, moonstone-pale with gold, octagonal or eight-bordered,
carrying rows of names. This is the option that actually says *Altmer ancestry* rather than
*elf-flavoured object*, and it is the one the lore supports most strongly.

## Naming

"Ancestral Focus" is already correct on the constraints that matter -- it names the inheritance
rather than a god, and it avoids colliding with the visible substrate tier `Ordered Heritage`. If we
move to the record framing, names worth considering: **Lineage Roll**, **Name-Roll**, **Kinhouse
Record**, **The Line Kept**. Owner's call; no change is needed for correctness.

## The calian -- the answer (added 2026-08-06)

A **calian** is a cultural talisman given to every Altmer on reaching majority at eighteen, in a
religious ceremony conducted by a Curate (an Ascendant Curate for a prestigious House; the High
Aldarch for royalty). It marks the bearer as **praxic** -- a participating member of Altmeri society.
It is a small, fragile sphere of "milky aetherquartz and sun-blown glass". When a Mer is judged
**apraxic**, their calian is symbolically **shattered**. Source: the ESO Summerset book *The Price of
Praxis* by Ceruval Rolumaril, quoted at length on the mod page below.

**Why it beats everything reconstructed above:** the lore says that without a calian a Mer "might as
well not exist". That is the same doctrine this document traced independently through the
genealogical records -- erasure from the record as a fate worse than death. Sections 1-2 argued the
Altmer devotional object should be *a token of being counted*, and reasoned toward a lineage roll.
The calian already IS that object, and it is canon.

Against this document's own constraints:

| Constraint | Calian |
|---|---|
| Deity-agnostic | PASS -- civic/social standing, not worship of a named god |
| Portable, works indoors | PASS -- personal, carried, needs no shrine |
| Not a relic of the dead | PASS -- concerns the LIVING mer's standing; avoids the Dunmer inversion entirely |
| Numerology-safe (never 2) | PASS -- a single sphere |

**It also retro-justifies a mechanic already shipped.** `EnsureAltmerPracticeFocus` silently grants
the token to any Altmer who lacks one. For a moonstone ingot that is arbitrary. For a calian it is
canon: every Altmer received one at eighteen.

**Vocabulary note.** The spine is defined as "the ordered life every Altmer keeps because they are
Altmer" -- which is *praxis*. Praxic/apraxic name the spine's two states more precisely than
"ancestral" does. Not a required change, but the better words now exist.

**The one honest extension.** The calian is attested as a marker of STANDING, not an instrument of
PRACTICE. Using it as a daily focus extends canon. Defensible -- it is conferred in a religious rite
after reciting the Athel Vialen, and its recipient "swore an oath to protect it", so practice as
oath-keeping follows naturally -- but it is an extension and is recorded here as one, not passed off
as lore.

**Future beat:** an apraxic Mer's calian is shattered. We already have an Altmer crisis state and an
Exiled path. A cracked variant swapped in at crisis or exile would be the most diegetic state marker
in the mod.

### BUILT 2026-08-06

| Surface | State |
|---|---|
| Item name | `0716E4` renamed **"Ancestral Focus" -> "Calian"** |
| Used again the same day | `071706 PDV_Notif_Altmer_Calian_AlreadyKept` -- "Your calian is already warm from today's practice." |
| Used while the Altmer curse suppresses favour | `071707 PDV_Notif_Altmer_Calian_Unanswered` -- "The calian does not warm to you now." |
| First granted | Book of Days entry, once ever, on the one-shot key `PDV.Altmer.Calian.Granted` |
| Inventory description | `PDV_Calian_DESC.ini` at the mod root (repo mirror: `mod-data/`) |

Both notification properties are declared on `PDV__ManagerQuest` and bound in the VMAD (indices 522
and 523, readback-verified non-null).

Three decisions worth keeping:

- **A non-Altmer holding the calian is told nothing.** It is not their object and there is no refusal
  to explain. The curse case IS explained, because that player owns the calian and needs to know the
  silence is their state rather than a broken item.
- **The granted line hangs off a one-shot key, not off the `AddItem`.** `EnsureAltmerPracticeFocus`
  re-grants the calian whenever the player does not have one, so tying the line to the grant would
  say "you have carried this since you were eighteen" about a replacement acquired a minute earlier.
- **The granted line is a Book of Days entry with `allowDuringRaceSetup = True`, not a
  notification.** The grant happens at init while race-setup presentation is suppressed, so a
  notification would be swallowed or would have to shout over the setup flow. It is backstory being
  entered in the chronicle, which is what the Book of Days is for.

### Mesh -- landed 2026-08-06, with one defect fixed on the way in

`0716E4 Model.File` is now `PDV\Clutter\PDV_AltmerCalian.nif` (was the moonstone ingot). The chosen
variant is **eggshell** from the white group -- the closest read on "milky aetherquartz". All 182
NIFs in the resource are the same geometry with a different embedded texture, so **changing colour
later is a one-field edit**, not a re-import.

**The resource ships with broken texture paths.** Its meshes reference
`MS03 Calians\textures\...`, but the files install to `textures\...`. Verified rather than assumed:
a vanilla control (`meshes\Dungeons\Mines\Ore\IngotMoonstone.nif`) uses the normal Data-relative
`textures\...` form, and an asset-status resolve of every `MS03 Calians\...` path returns **ABSENT**
while the `textures\...` equivalents resolve fine. As installed, those calians render untextured.
This is the author's packaging, not something we introduced -- worth reporting upstream.

**How we handled it.** Devotion ships the four textures at the paths its mesh already expects, so
the item is self-contained and carries no dependency on the resource mod:

```
meshes\PDV\Clutter\PDV_AltmerCalian.nif
MS03 Calians\textures\white\eggshell.dds
MS03 Calians\textures\cubemaps\shinyglass_e.dds
MS03 Calians\textures\calianjewelrybox.dds
MS03 Calians\textures\calianjewelrybox_n.dds
```

All five resolve with Devotion as the winning provider (asset-status verified).

**Why that odd top-level folder name.** The first attempt was the tidy one -- repath the mesh's six
texture slots to `textures\PDV\Clutter\...` via `housecarl_nif_set`. It **refused and wrote
nothing**: its verification saw a block change the edit did not claim. Rather than push through six
sequential edits and risk a half-repathed mesh, the textures ship where the mesh already looks. The
folder name is inherited from the resource, not chosen.

**Cleanup available later:** repath the six slots in NifSkope, move the textures under
`textures\PDV\Clutter\`, and the `MS03 Calians` folder can be deleted. Cosmetic only.

**OPEN -- worth your eyes.** The mesh has THREE shapes: two glass shells (`Box02:10` at scale 1.2 and
`Box02:0` at 1.3, the second alpha-tested, which is how the translucency is faked) and one
`BarenziahsJewelryBox:0` textured from the vanilla jewelry-box atlas. That third shape is visible
(default flags), so the item may render as a sphere in or on a wooden box rather than a bare sphere.
For a talisman carried on the person a bare sphere is probably wanted. Removing a shape is geometry,
which houseCARL does not touch -- that is a NifSkope job.
